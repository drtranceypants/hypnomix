The web app. One command opens it in your browser; it runs on your computer only. Choose a Live set, a REAPER project, an Ardour session, or a folder of recordings from any DAW (or paste its path: the installed Windows app has no file chooser, because it ships without Python's Tk, so there you paste the path into the Path box), and press one button: it runs every check and builds the before-and-after clips. The review shows the fixes you can hear first, by severity, each with one player and A and B buttons that swap the clip at the same moment, and three choices: Approve, Skip, Change. You can do the whole review at the keyboard (j and k move, a, s and c decide, space plays, b switches A and B, [ and ] jump to the previous or next undecided one, ? lists the keys), and a click changes only its own card, so the clip you are hearing keeps playing. The bar counts what you approved and how many of those you never played. Approve all low and Approve the N like this show you the whole list first and record nothing until you confirm; the first never includes tuning or other taste calls, tidy steps, or protected tracks. Items hypnomix cannot do itself wait in a By hand in your DAW section, and a filter row narrows the list by decision or tool. A long review (more than 150 findings) opens with its first group built and every other big group behind a button that shows its full count; a group builds a hundred more cards as you scroll to its end or press its button, and the keyboard builds the next card as it reaches it (the counts in the bar and in the group headings always cover all of the findings). A note you add changes only its own card. A few tidy-up steps (track order, removing switched-off tracks, joining one instrument onto one track) are on by default: they say so on their cards, run unless you press Turn off, and are listed on the Apply card before the button. Apply writes only what you approved into a new file next to your original (a new .als, .rpp or .ardour), says in plain words which file names it will create and which approved items are by hand and will not be applied, then shows what was applied, what was not and why, where to listen, and an Undo button where one exists (Live sets). Make the handoff list turns the by-hand items into a checklist with a marker at each spot. Re-scan repeats a scan on the song as it is now, and the review says so if the project was saved after the scan. While a scan runs, the page shows how many of its checks are done and which are still running, with an estimate once the song has been scanned before (never a percentage). The Activity log keeps the newest 4,000 lines of a long command, and says how many older ones it left out. Your preferences and every other hypnomix tool are a click away, each with a form. The page remembers the last song you chose and the review you were on.

The Live device. A Max for Live device you drop on any track; audio passes through it untouched. It shows the finding you are on and steps through them, and Live jumps to each one: the track is selected, the playhead moves to the spot, the loop brace goes around it and the clip opens. Approve or skip right there. "Show in Live" buttons in the app do the same jump. Press Float (top right of the device) and the same panel opens in a small floating window: a jump selects the finding's track, so Live shows that track's clip where the device was, and the floating panel stays in view. The device never edits the set it sits in: applying still happens in the app, into a new set.

DAW mode. hypnomix app --daw makes the app the whole Hypnomix DAW: it opens on the DAW tab (Arrangement, Session, Mixer, Review and the Assistant) and runs the Hypnomix engine underneath, starting it again if it stops. The engine comes only with the full download, which is not released yet, so an ordinary install has no DAW tab until an engine is found or the app is started with --daw. DAW mode.

The Teacher tab. A guided course in your own DAW: a tour of hypnomix and its devices if you already engineer, a 101 crash course (recording to a click, each effect, mastering), or the complete course A to Z (138 to 146 lessons depending on your DAW, from what sound is to releasing a song, with audio examples, quizzes and a glossary) if you're new to audio. It can do the hands-on steps in Live and REAPER for you, asking first each time. Teacher mode.

The Wiki. Every lesson, how-to guide, command and FAQ answer on this site is also in the app's Wiki tab, with search, and it works offline. When the website has newer articles, the app shows those: it only downloads the articles, and you can switch that off in the tab. While the Wiki is in use it asks the website at most once an hour, saying which copy it already has, so a list that has not changed costs a few hundred bytes and one that has comes compressed; the Wiki tab reads that list once when you open it, not again for each article. Whatever comes from the website is cleaned before it is shown: scripts, forms, frames and drawings are removed, and a link has to be a web address, an email address or a place inside the wiki. Only the wiki's own styling names are kept, so an article cannot pose as one of the app's buttons, a website copy that is damaged is skipped (the app's own articles show instead), and an article longer than 1 MB is cut short. Pictures, videos and sounds are downloaded only in the usual kinds and up to 50 MB each; any other file is offered as a download and never opened in the app.

The Store tab. Free and open-source plug-ins, synths, drum kits and sample packs, each with its licence and source, installed with one click from the publisher's own download. Nothing downloaded is run: installer packages open for you to run yourself. Mac only for now: on another system the tab says so. The store.

The Assistant and the Listener (optional). Rather talk than click? The Assistant tab is a chat with the AI of your choice that runs hypnomix for you, and a Listener, a Google model with its own key, can hear a bounce and give notes by ear. Both use your own keys, and each listen waits for your click. How to set them up.

Both run on your computer only. Nothing about your songs is sent anywhere unless you set up a Listener and click Send it, and the app refuses requests from other sites. The device needs Max for Live (Live Suite, or the add-on). It has been loaded and used in Live 12 Suite on a real song; it is new, so tell us what breaks.

New: measure every track while Live plays, tell hypnomix what each track is and what the song is, A/B every fix inside Live with a blind test, build an editable mastering chain of Live’s own devices that matches your loudness target, and record a review before export. All of it has been tested in Live 12. See the lessons in this wiki for step-by-step pictures and videos.

The Mac and Windows app

The easiest way to get it: the installer for Mac or Windows from the download page. On a Mac it's Hypnomix.app, a window of its own; on Windows the Start menu opens it in your browser. The installer is the Hypnomix Assistant, for Live, REAPER and Ardour; the full Hypnomix with its own DAW built in is not released yet, so there is nothing to add to it for now (Setup → Extras says what it is). Its engine is a separate program under the GNU General Public License (version 2 or later), a rebranded fork of Ardour; hypnomix itself stays MIT. The first time it opens, a guided setup asks which of the two you want, how you'd like to work (on your own, with an AI key, with a free model on your computer, or with Claude Code), which DAW you use and whether you'd like teacher mode, and shows the costs and the pros and cons of each: Setting Up, and Choosing an AI.

Installed from the source? You can build the same Mac app yourself in about a minute, made for (and trusted by) your own Mac:

1. Install Apple's command line tools once, if you haven't: xcode-select --install.
2. In the hypnomix folder, run macapp/build.sh.
3. Open macapp/dist/Hypnomix.app, or drag it into Applications.

If you move or reinstall the hypnomix folder, run the build again. The web app and the Mac app are the same thing inside; use whichever you like.

$ xcode-select --install     # once
$ cd hypnomix && macapp/build.sh
$ open macapp/dist/Hypnomix.app
