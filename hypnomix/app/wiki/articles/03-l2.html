Before your first review, let HypnoMix study the songs you've already finished. It reads every Live set on your computer (it skips backups, factory packs and older versions of the same song) and your exported bounces. Nothing is rendered and nothing leaves your Mac. A few hundred sets take well under a minute, and after that only the sets you've changed are read again.

What it learns: how loud you set each kind of track against the lead vocal, how wide you pan, how much reverb and delay each part gets, which devices and plug-ins you use on what, how you set your compressors, EQs and limiters, what you automate, and how loud and bright your released songs are. A setting you keep the same in every song becomes a standard that tools stick to. One that changes from song to song is a taste call, and you'll be asked about it.

$ hypnomix taste --library                   # every set on this computer
$ hypnomix taste --library "~/Music/Projects"  # or just one folder
→ LIBRARY.md
# Your style, learned from your library
Learned from 9 sets in 24 s; 341 .als files found, 332 skipped (backups, packs, older versions).
## YOUR STYLE
- harmony: -5.9 dB against the lead (songs from -8.5 to -2.3 dB) · taste call
- centred in every song: bass, drums, harmony, lead · standard
- lead: reverb send -14.2 dB; delay send -12 dB
- bass: Compressor, Utility, EQ Eight (3 songs)
## WHERE YOU COULD IMPROVE
1. Bounces peak above -1 dBTP: three releases at -0.07, -0.5 and -0.62 dBTP.
   Streaming encodes add 0.5-1 dB, so they may clip after upload: try a -1 dB ceiling.
2. Compressor release faster than two bass cycles: 30 ms on a bass whose lowest
   note needs 61 ms. Try a longer release so it doesn't distort the low end.
3. Loudness differs by 4 LU between your songs: choose one target for the album.

Not sure of a song's genre? It guesses and asks you to confirm: hypnomix taste --library --genre "Song=folk". Once genres are set, your style is also broken down by genre.
