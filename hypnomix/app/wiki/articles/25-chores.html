Hunting clicks, pops and level jumpsFinds clicks at clip edges and loop seams, jumps where one comped clip cuts to the next, plosives and spikes, and eases each with a short fade or dip. It also catches the opposite mistake: a fade or comp edit that cuts into a word.smooth edges clicks
Finding the hit that's lateDetects the song's own grid (straight, triplets or a mix), locks the low end first (bass to the drums), compares every hit with the rest of the band, and nudges only the clear outliers part of the way. Drums go first, then the bass toward the corrected drums, then everything else. Audio gets warp markers you can still drag; MIDI drums and bass are checked too. One pass of a loop can be nudged on its own.timing tighten unroll
Tuning a vocal note by noteMeasures the song's real tuning and key (section by section), then moves only each note's centre. Vibrato, scoops and slides stay as sung. It checks the chord under every note, and asks when the target clashes with it or the key is unsure; those notes stay as sung until you answer.tune key
Lining up doubles and harmoniesWorks word by word: only the words clearly out of time with the other parts move, the rest stay bit-exact. Optional pitch matching (a harmony keeps its interval, the chord is checked) and formant for width. In Live it writes warp markers on your own clips; in REAPER, stretch markers or new takes on your own items; always into a new set or project. The same from inside Live (the Hypnomix Align device) or from the HypnoMix Align panel in REAPER.vocalign alignset aligndevice reaper align
Balancing a harmony stackLists each part with its note and level, asks which one leads, and turns down only the parts that overshadow it, by the amount you pick or the spacing you use in your own finished songs. It is judged as heard, after each track's effects, and written as volume automation.voices cliplevel
Keeping the vocal level even, line to lineMeasures the vocal as heard, one lyric line at a time: a line that drops away or jumps out, and an upper harmony louder than the melody. Run it in the first pass and again before every final export, because edits since then can change it. Fixes are written as volume automation into a new set, only if you ask: approving one in the review writes nothing by itself, vocallevel --apply does. In REAPER the same, as volume envelope points in a new project (opened and read back on a two-track test project in REAPER 7.86 (2026-11-26), not on a real song and not judged by ear). The scan runs this check when there are two or more vocal tracks. Nothing existing is written over, and a track whose envelope would go above +6 dB is left for you to change by hand.vocallevel
Chasing hiss, hum, breaths and "s" soundsSpectral de-hiss, hum and rumble removal, breaths turned down (not off), a split-band de-esser that lists every "s" it touched, clipped peaks rebuilt. cleanup rescues a rough demo with the same gentle hand.prep dehiss deess breaths cleanup
Evening out clips recorded on different daysMatches the loudness and tone of clips recorded at different preamp gain or mic distance, so a part sounds like one performance. On files, or on a track in your Live set, where it can set each clip's Clip Gain.clipmatch
Setting your level, picking the takeListens to your interface input and says how to set the preamp before you record, with a live meter. Afterwards it ranks takes by clipping, noise, hum, pitch and timing and writes a comp list: the best take for each section. The numbers find problems; you pick the performance.soundcheck takes
Finding what's distortingClipped takes, drive set too hot, limiters working too hard, compressors releasing faster than a bass note: each named with its track, bar and a setting to try.distortion
Setting up sidechains and duckingMeasures how deep each sidechain ducks and whether it recovers in time, proposes settings for the style you want, and can make the vocal reverb and delay step back while the singer sings. The sidechain check reads Live sets, REAPER projects and HypnoMix Studio sessions; duck works on Live sets and HypnoMix Studio sessions (there it writes into the open engine only with --engine --ids).sidechain duck
Naming, ordering and tidying 40 tracksNames every track by how it sounds, puts them in working order (drums, bass, keys and guitars, vocals) and puts each harmony on the track for its pitch, so one fader is one voice. Switched-off old takes are retired after a full archive copy of the set is saved, and one instrument's clips go on one track. On a HypnoMix Studio session, order and tidy propose groups instead, written only with --engine --ids.name order tidy
Checking every compressor and gateIn a Live set, plays each track through its own chain and judges every stock device on the signal it really gets: a compressor that barely works, squashes, or lets go too fast for a bass note; a gate that chops words. It suggests threshold, attack and release for that part and tempo. In a REAPER project it judges each ReaComp the same way, on the signal of its own track, and fix writes the suggested threshold, ratio, attack and release into a new project (a ReaComp written that way read back exactly on a two-track test project in REAPER 7.86 (2026-11-26), not on a real song and not judged by ear). ReaGate and ReaLimit are listed, not judged: their saved settings are not read. A ReaComp with Wet at 0, or with dry and wet mixed (parallel compression), is listed and not judged, and gets no fix; plug-ins on the master track are not read. In a HypnoMix Studio session each ACE Compressor is judged the same way (its Range, Auto Release and Mix included), with the settings listed to set by hand; ACE EQ's High Pass band counts as a low cut, and a hard-driven Hypnomix Saturator gets a lower Drive suggested. In a Live set a Compressor or Glue Compressor with Dry/Wet under 97 % is parallel compression: listed with what you hear, not judged.devices
Mapping a take played without a clickFollows the beat through the take, writes a click check for your ears, then either warps the take onto the grid or makes Live's tempo follow the player. It also picks the best loops and lines them up to try.tempomap loops
Making a looped song feel aliveSmall tempo pushes into choruses, filter sweeps and delay throws in the style of your own automation, and unused takes or loops from the original project offered on new, switched-off tracks. Live sets (loops and variations also fill HypnoMix Studio's clip slots).breathe motion variations borrow
A song that sounds the same throughoutFinds the parts that repeat themselves section after section (measured: tone and texture) and proposes a new colour for the repeat: width, grit, space, movement or an octave copy. Each is a chain of Live's own devices on a switched-off copy of the part, with a before and after clip. Live sets only for now.colour
Working out which parts clashWhich two parts fight over which frequencies, how often, and the exact cut that fixes it, written once you approve it as a band on the part's EQ, or on a new EQ added to its chain if it has none (EQ Eight in Live, ReaEQ in REAPER). Mic pairs checked for phase and polarity.masking align stems
Stems in, song outGive it a folder of stems: it cleans them, works out what each one is, EQs, compresses, de-esses, balances, pans, adds one shared reverb and masters the result. MIX NOTES.md explains every move in plain words. With --plan, every move becomes a finding you approve first.automix
A first balance of a folder of stemsOne fader move per part, with the measurement behind it: its role, how loud it plays, and how the vocal sits against the band, compared with your own fader range when you have a profile. Faders only: no EQ or compression. Anchor part, vocal level and per-role offsets are yours to set.balance
Getting loud without wrecking itA mastering chain set by measurement, test-encoded to AAC and MP3 so it won't clip after upload, built offline or inside Live from stock devices, or as an editable rack on HypnomixMain that you save as your own template.master chain
Mastering to your soundTarget tone ranges built from your own releases or records you choose; a tonal stabilizer, dynamic EQ, width by band, limiter character, harshness taming, dither and an overall amount. Turn the vocal, drums or bass up or down in a finished mix, true-peak safe. Hear it as MP3 or AAC. Match a reference with Matchering (an extra).tonal harsh rebalance reference
The pre-release checklistPhone, earbuds, car and mono checks; how much each streaming service will turn it down; silence, true peak, phase and format. Section by section: "the second chorus is 2 LU quieter than the first". Your mix against a song you love, as proposals.translate qc sections match
Packaging the releaseOne folder to upload: your master as 24-bit WAV (untouched), a dithered 16-bit WAV, MP3 320 and AAC 256 with tags and cover, and a report that measures every file, including the true peak after the lossy ones are decoded. --check first.release
Instrumental, a cappella, TV mix, radio editFrom your own exported stems, no separation. Checks the stems really add up to your mix, matches each version to its loudness with gain only, crossfades the cuts of a radio edit (to bar lines with --bpm) and runs the release checks on every file.versions
Is the new bounce better than the last one?Lines the two up and says in plain words what changed: loudness, peaks, dynamics, tone and stereo width by band, each section, with level-matched A/B clips of the three spots that differ most.compare
Making an EP or album sit togetherThe songs side by side in plain words: which sit apart in level (judged on the loudest section as well as the whole, so a quiet ballad may be quieter), which are brighter, darker or boomier and by how much, the gap each pair could have, and order options. Any trim, gentle tilt or silence change is a finding you approve, applied into new files.album
Checking the files before you startMixed sample rates or bit depths and which file is the odd one out, stems of different length or that start apart, duplicates, fake stereo, silent or clipped files, lossy sources, missing files, names that will not travel. Report first; prep repairs what it can.preflight
A second opinion by earA cloud AI model listens to a bounce and writes notes with timestamps, checked against HypnoMix's own measurements. It needs your own key, sends a reduced mono copy, and only when you say so (critique shows what it will send and asks y/N first). Its notes are an opinion; the numbers stay measurements.critique
Splitting a song, or turning audio into notesSplits a finished song into stems, or turns audio into MIDI (and into a Live clip). Both are optional extras and run on your computer.separate midi
Learning the loop on nothing of your ownA 40-second synthetic song with six labelled problems (a click, a level jump, hiss, clipped peaks, a drifting harmony, late hits), as stems or a REAPER project. The REAPER project (demo --kind reaper) is the one that gives the full loop: scan it, hear each fix, apply two. The folder of stems has fewer fixes you can apply.demo
Finding every problem in a song at onceRuns every check on a Live set, a REAPER project, a HypnoMix Studio session or a folder of recordings, changes nothing, and writes one review list, most severe first. What looks deliberate (your automation, a long reverb tail) is listed as a question, never fixed on its own. On a REAPER project it also checks the file itself (the structure group): that it reads back exactly as saved, that folders close, that every send comes from a track that exists, and faders at the top or nearly silent. It only lists these; nothing is changed.scan
Hearing a fix before you say yesA before and after clip for every fix it can preview, level-matched, so the louder one does not win. In Live, every fix as a switched-off B next to your own device (A / B / Blind), or offline through your own VST3 and AU plug-ins.ab abset plugrender
Deciding 100+ findingsAmounts per fix, the same decision for the ones like this, a question budget, why each technique, notes and a collaborator's review merged. fix writes only what you approved into one new set, and every version is kept and can be undone.review fix history
What's left to do by handTurns the by-hand list into a checklist in your DAW's words, in song order, with what to listen for, and puts a marker at each spot: locators in a new Live set, markers in a new REAPER project, or a MIDI and CSV marker file for any other DAW.handoff
Re-checking after every changeA quick scan in seconds, only the tracks you changed, or on every save.watch
Learning how you mixAsks your targets once, reads your own finished songs for your standards and taste calls, and shows where you could improve, with the evidence. Once you have built a profile, scan --mix also compares the mix with your own ranges (--no-profile skips it). Everything stays on your computer.prefs taste profile
Knowing which plug-ins you haveLists what is installed (VST3, Audio Units, CLAP, LV2), marks the free ones HypnoMix knows, loads each one safely (a plug-in that crashes or hangs is reported, not fatal) and keeps the list in your preferences so proposals only name what you have.plugins
Measuring every track while you playA Max for Live probe last on each track measures it after all its plug-ins; one play of the song is a deep scan, no renders or exports.probe

Reporting a problemWrites a bug report, or the record of a crash, on your computer and shows it to you first, with your paths, names and keys hidden as far as HypnoMix can tell (read it before you share it). Copy it, save it, or open the project's issue page and paste it yourself: HypnoMix has no server and never sends anything. See Reporting a problem.report

What stays yours: the playing, the sounds, the effects, the arrangement and every taste call. HypnoMix asks before anything that changes how the song feels, shows what is deliberate (your automation, your long reverb tail) on every review so nobody "fixes" it, and never overwrites a file.
