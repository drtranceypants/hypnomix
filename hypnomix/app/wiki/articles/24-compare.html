After every round of changes, compare a full export with the previous one: did the loudness move, did the top end change, did the second chorus lose its lift? compare answers that for two bounces of the same song and writes it as COMPARE.md.

$ hypnomix compare "Song v3.wav" "Song v4.wav" --names "v3,v4"
$ hypnomix compare old.wav new.wav --bpm 96 --out ~/Desktop/v3-v4

It opens with one line, like "v4 is 1.2 dB louder, 1.5 dB brighter above 8 kHz, a little narrower in the lows; the 2nd chorus-like section changed most", and then the numbers behind it.

  • Lined up first. If one file starts later (an export with extra silence at the front), it finds by how much, to the sample, and says so. Only the part both share is compared.
  • Loudness and peaks: integrated, the loudest 10 seconds, true peak. Dynamics: crest, peak-to-loudness, loudness range.
  • Tone by band (8 bands, sub to air) as each band's share of the song, so loudness does not blur it: which got brighter or darker, by how many dB. Stereo width by band, wider or narrower.
  • Loudness by section, from sections (give --bpm if its tempo is wrong).
  • Where they differ most: three spots, each as a level-matched pair, A (the first file) and B (the second), in ab/ next to the report. ab/ab.json is the list review reads, so the same players work. If B is a little louder you still hear tone and balance, not volume.

It stops politely when the two do not look like the same song (--force compares anyway), when a file is silent or under two seconds, or when the sample rates differ (--resample brings the second to the first's rate). Neither file is changed, and an existing report is never overwritten.

Measured, not heard: use the A / B clips, and trust your ears over the numbers. See also match (a mix against a reference) and sections.
