What it is. HypnoMix Studio is HypnoMix's own DAW: an optional add-on that you choose in Setup, and that the app opens as its Studio tab (DAW mode). It is built on Ardour, a free open-source DAW under the GNU General Public License (version 2 or later). The add-on carries that licence with it, its source is public at github.com/drtranceypants/hypnomix-daw, and HypnoMix itself stays MIT. Ardour is the engine under the surface, and this page is where HypnoMix says so.

HypnoMix Studio is new: its first engine (0.1.0) is for Apple-silicon Macs. Steps written here for it were written against Ardour 8 or 9, the free DAW it is built on, and not every one has been tried in HypnoMix Studio yet. There is no engine for Intel Macs or Windows yet. Two things below are the exception and are marked as such: the mastering chain built over MCP, and the checks made with the engine; both need HypnoMix's own build of the engine, which stock Ardour does not have.

Its sessions. A HypnoMix Studio session is an Ardour-format session folder with a .ardour file in it. Sessions saved by Ardour 3 to 9 open the same way, so everything below applies to them as well. HypnoMix reads these sessions and writes new snapshots of them without the DAW having to be open.

What works today. HypnoMix reads HypnoMix Studio sessions (.ardour): tracks and buses, regions (with the way a session layers them: upper regions cover lower ones), fade curves, gain envelopes, multi-channel regions, plug-ins and automation. It renders each track from the session without opening the DAW, and scan runs the same checks as on a REAPER project: missing files, clicks at region edges, level jumps at comp cuts, vocal pitch and doubles, plug-ins switched off, the source recordings, boosted noise and "is this deliberate?" questions.

Tempo maps. A session's tempo map is read exactly: steady tempos, and tempo ramps as HypnoMix Studio plays them (the tempo changes evenly beat by beat from one point to the next), so a region placed in music time after a ramp is read at the right second.

Automation and tempo from HypnoMix's commands. vocallevel writes its line-to-line moves as automation (the upper parts' trims on their trim, the line evening on the vocal bus's fader only when it carries nothing but vocals), motion its filter sweeps on the session's own filter plug-ins, and tempomap --follow and breathe a tempo map (ramps included). Each takes --engine (into the session open in HypnoMix Studio: one undo step each, shown as unsaved until you save) or --apply NAME.ardour (a new snapshot beside the session, structure-checked). Level moves keep to the smooth-automation rules: gentle S-curves of about 1.2 s, at most 3 dB a move (a trim 4 dB), never steeper than 7 dB/s, never on a bus that also carries non-vocal tracks. Audio keeps its time when the tempo changes, so breathe asks for --grid-only when the session has audio.

Takes. A track's other playlists named <track>.take N are read as its takes (HypnoMix Studio records each pass into one and comps from them). They are listed like Live's take lanes and never heard: the track plays its active playlist, the comp. The scan says which tracks are comped from takes; its checks and fixes work on the comp's own regions (each still plays its take's file, so the edges check can look past a region's edge into the take), and the takes are never written. A missing file is said once: as a problem when the comp plays it, and as a smaller one (you cannot comp from that take) when only a take uses it.

Fixes are written into a new snapshot. hypnomix fix "hypnomix-out/scan/Song" --approved --out "Song (fixed).ardour" writes one new .ardour with the changes you approved, next to your session file, inside the same session folder. That is where the DAW keeps its own snapshots, and where it looks for the session's audio (interchange/<session>/audiofiles), so the new file finds the same recordings; nothing is copied or moved, and your original is never touched. It works on both time formats a session can use (sample times in older sessions, superclock and beat times in newer ones).

What it can write: region fades in and out (as a curve; shape=linear, power or slow in the decision note; a fade that is already longer is left alone), region gain, a dip over a spike (a quick turn-down in the region's gain envelope, so your fader and automation stay yours), the eased level after a comp cut (a gain envelope on the region that starts a little quieter and returns to normal; method=item sets the region gain instead), and fader, pan and mute on a track. A fader, pan or mute that already plays automation is refused. Track names and order, vocal stacks, timing nudges (each timing finding says how to do it by hand in the DAW: split around the hit and slide it, or stretch the region) and sidechain settings stay by hand for now, and so does tuning (those make new audio you bring in). hypnomix handoff turns the by-hand list into a checklist in the DAW's words, with a MIDI marker file and a CSV to import.

Safety. It refuses when the session was saved after the scan, when the output exists, or on protected tracks. Each approved finding is found again by its region; one that is not there is skipped and said so. The new file is checked: same tracks, buses, regions, positions and lengths, ids unique, fade and envelope times well-formed, regions inside their files, and nothing outside the approved edits differs. A file that fails is renamed "(BROKEN, do not open)". Then fix reads it back, lists exactly what changed and measures the clips again.

Written and re-read by HypnoMix; not yet opened in a released DAW. HypnoMix Studio is new, and Ardour is not installed where this was built, so no fix snapshot has been opened in a released DAW yet. The reader was tested on real session files saved by Ardour 3, 8 and 9; the writer on synthetic sessions in both time formats. Please check in the DAW you use, and tell us what you see:

  • the new snapshot opens without a warning (Session > Open, or the snapshot list);
  • the audio is found (no missing-files dialog);
  • fades and gain are as written: the listed regions show the fade lengths and curve, the gain, and the envelope dips;
  • pan direction is the way round you expect (azimuth 0 is left), if you approved a pan move;
  • the trim knob is where it was (the fader move leaves it alone).

Findings that make new audio (tuning, de-hiss, de-ess) work as for any DAW. Driving the open session with the engine's own plug-ins is next on the roadmap. The reader's renders also haven't been compared with the DAW's own export.

Mastering in HypnoMix Studio. In HypnoMix Studio chain master --ardour builds the mastering chain in the open session over MCP: a high-pass and low-pass filter, an EQ and a compressor from the engine's own plug-ins, then HypnoMix's own utility (gain, width, mono, bass mono with a phase-coherent crossover, polarity, DC filter) and its limiter (look-ahead, holds a true-peak ceiling measured with 4x oversampling), every value dialled in from your bounce. Not there yet: a saturator, --verify, and chain strips. If a step fails part-way, the build is taken back out: what this run added is removed, your own plug-ins are switched on again (with --existing bypass they are only switched off once the whole chain is in), and the message says what was undone. A stage that could not be built or set reads NOT BUILT or NOT SET in the table, a value the plug-in's range had to move reads CLAMPED with the planned and the set value (for gain, ceiling, threshold and make-up, which change the loudness, it also counts as not set), and the command exits with an error. After an incomplete chain, take the plug-ins it added off the master strip before running it again, or it would build the chain twice (the command refuses to run on top of them unless you pass --existing). The engine's address must be on this computer. This needs HypnoMix's own build of the engine (its MCP surface and its utility and limiter plug-ins are not in stock Ardour), and it was tried against a stand-in for that MCP surface, not against the running engine.

$ hypnomix chain master "Song bounce.wav" --target -14 --ardour --existing bypass   # the chain on the master bus

Groups and sidechains in HypnoMix Studio. With the session open in HypnoMix Studio, groups are written straight into it, but only the ones you name with --ids (nothing is written without it; in the DAW view, Group and Ungroup ask first). hypnomix tidy "Song.ardour" offers one folded Old takes group track for the switched-off tracks (in a Live set that still needs Live: select them and press ⌘G), and hypnomix order "Song.ardour" groups each family of instruments that sits in no group yet (two or more drum tracks become Drums, vocals Vocals, and so on, judged by the last instrument word in the name, so Bass Drum goes with the drums and Synth Bass with the bass, and a few two-word names are known as a whole, such as Kick Sub (drums) and Bass Synth (bass); a track already routed into a bus is left alone). Bars are counted from the session's time signature (6/8 is three quarter notes a bar), and a session whose time signature changes gets a warning that its later bar numbers are not exact. Add --engine --ids group-1 to make the ones you approve (a group whose tracks have different channel counts is listed as by hand and never written): each is a route group plus its bus, read back from the engine. The engine must have that very session file open: a copy of the same name in another folder, another session, or an engine that names no saved file is refused and nothing is written, as is an id that is not among the options listed (the engine's address for --engine URL is in the DAW view's engine panel). HypnoMix sees the change: the session shows as unsaved, and Stop, Open, New and quit ask Save first. The engine cannot undo a group: Ungroup puts it back. The sidechain check (in the scan, or hypnomix sidechain "Song.ardour") measures every ACE Compressor keyed from another track or bus hit by hit and gives the Threshold, Ratio, Attack, Release and Knee to set (a compressor that is switched off, or whose key send is switched off, is said, not measured; a bus as the key is heard as its tracks at their faders); hypnomix duck "Song.ardour" looks at the vocal reverb and delay returns and, with --engine --ids duck-1, adds an ACE Compressor at the end of a return keyed from the vocals, its threshold fitted to what the pre-fader key carries (so the vocal fader does not change it), or switches on and retunes one that is already there (after checking that each track id, and a tuned compressor, are still the ones the scan saw); every value is read back from the engine, and a control the engine does not take is reported as not done. These were checked against a development build of the engine on its silent test backend, with a made-up session (the keyed compressor's gain reduction was read while it played), and against a stand-in engine in the unit tests: not on a real song, not through a sound card, and not by ear.

Bring a Live set across: hypnomix als2ardour. hypnomix als2ardour "Song.als" "Song (HypnoMix)" makes a new session folder from a Live set; the set and its project folder are only read, and the new folder must be somewhere else. It is built in a hidden folder next to it and renamed into place at the end, so a failure leaves nothing half-made (a hidden .NAME.tmp-… folder left by a force-quit can be deleted). What comes across: every track with its name, order and colour; the Arrangement's clips as regions in the same places, with their clip gain, fades (Live's short edge fades too), switched-off clips muted and looped clips laid out one region per pass; faders, pans, track on/off (as mute), solo and their automation; return tracks as buses with an aux send from each track at the same level (pre or post as in Live); group tracks as route groups, with a bus the group's tracks feed when the group has its own devices, fader, sends or automation; the tempo and time signature, and tempo changes as tempo points (a tempo ramp becomes a run of small exact steps, so the map agrees with itself). The audio is copied into the session (one mono file per channel, as the DAW's own import does; a file at another sample rate is resampled), so the session never depends on the Live project. MIDI clips bring their notes, but not the instrument.

Warped clips. A clip whose warp markers make one straight line plays at one speed: at the set's tempo (most recorded audio) it comes across exactly; at another speed it comes across as a stretched copy of the file (Rubber Band, pitch kept), and the report says so. Clips whose warp markers bend the timing are not carried as warps yet (a session has no warp maps; that is the next step): they are placed along the straight line from their first to last marker and listed with how far that drifts.

What it never guesses. Devices and plug-ins are not carried: IMPORT-REPORT.md, next to the new .ardour, lists every track's Live devices (and the main track's), the take lanes and Session clips left behind, missing files, resampled files, samples from outside the project folder, and the Live settings that change what you hear and are not carried: a clip's transpose, the Re-Pitch warp mode, track delay, split stereo pan, clip grooves, the crossfader's A/B side, fade curve shapes and crossfades, MIDI note probability and controller envelopes, time-signature changes and locators. The new session is built next to the folder you name (inside it, if it is an empty folder you made) and moved into place only when it is complete, so a failure leaves nothing half-made and a folder that already exists empty stays the same folder. Only real audio files are copied (the file's own contents are checked, not its name), and a set from a newer Live than HypnoMix has been tested with prints one warning line. Until the processing is rebuilt the session plays the raw clips through the faders, so it will not sound like the Live mix. Live's pan law differs from the session's off-centre, and track names lose the characters a session does not allow (| : / ? * become _). A track inside a group whose output Live sends straight to the Main track skips the group here too. Copied files and MIDI files always get names of their own, so two takes called Vox.wav and vox.wav never overwrite each other.

How it was checked. On a real 31-track set (883 regions, groups, returns, MIDI): the als2ardour session was opened in HypnoMix's own build of the engine (a development build, not a release; stock Ardour does not have the MCP surface used for this check) and every region was where the set has it (read back over its MCP); a headless engine loaded and saved it and it read back the same (MIDI positions within a microsecond); and hypnomix project --render of the new session matched hypnomix render of the set track for track: same timing to the sample, same level within 0.001 dB. Played by the engine itself (hypnomix9-export, the lead vocal alone), the new session matched hypnomix render of the set to the sample, residual 97 dB down. Not yet checked: that it sounds right when played in the app, clip volume envelopes against Live's own playback, and a set with tempo ramps opened in HypnoMix Studio itself (the written tempo map was only checked against Live's own integral; the report names the ramps and how far a point inside one can be off).

Bring a project from another DAW across: hypnomix import. hypnomix import "Song.logicx" "Song (HypnoMix)" makes a new session folder from a project made in another DAW (or Import in the DAW view's engine panel). Your project is never changed and nothing is overwritten: the new folder must not exist yet or be empty, and must not be inside the project. The audio is copied in, and only audio inside the project is taken: to take audio the project points to elsewhere (a shared media folder), tick Also copy audio from outside the project in the app, or add --allow-outside (without it, such files are listed and left out). A .als set is the exception: it goes through als2ardour, which copies its samples wherever they are and lists the ones from outside its project folder. Before the new session is kept, HypnoMix reads it back with its own session reader and checks that every track and region is there. IMPORT-REPORT.md beside the new session lists what was left out or changed (audio not found, files outside the project, values out of range) and what the reader knew it could not carry. A .als set goes through als2ardour, above. .ptx sessions and .aaf exports are read by two small readers that come with the HypnoMix Studio add-on, which is for Apple-silicon Macs only so far: without it those two cannot be imported.

After the import. 1. Read IMPORT-REPORT.md beside the new session: what was left out (audio not found, files from outside the project, values out of range) and what the file type could not carry. 2. Open the new session (the app offers to). 3. If you hear nothing, connect the Master to your outputs once and save. 4. Add the plug-ins and instruments listed per track: their settings never come across. 5. Put back by hand what the report lists, then save; your original project is unchanged.

Limits. So that a damaged or hostile file cannot fill the memory or the disk, an import stops in one sentence, with nothing written, past 2,000 tracks, 20,000 regions or 500,000 MIDI notes (whichever comes first). A .dawproject's project.xml may be 32 MB and 2 million elements, with no DTD or entities, and clips nested at most 32 deep. These are left out with a line instead, and the rest imports: an audio file inside a .dawproject over 8 GB, one that unpacks to more than 200x its packed size (digital silence: past 16 MB, alone or with the files before it), and audio that would take more than 40% of the free disk. A file that must be converted (another sample rate, or a format other than WAV, AIFF, FLAC or Ogg) is taken up to 4 hours long and up to 8x resampling; a looped region repeats at most 256 times (a loop under 50 ms is placed once); a tempo map keeps 5,000 points.

How import was checked. Checked: .als through als2ardour's own tests; .logicx decoded from real packages saved by version 10.7, with fader values, mutes, plug-in names and region places compared with the window image each package stores of itself (the program that made them was not run), and one of them imported, opened in the HypnoMix engine on its silent test backend and its tracks, faders and regions read back; .dawproject, .rpp, .ptx and .aaf only with files made by the tests or stand-in readers. No imported session has been opened in a released DAW.

$ hypnomix project "Song/"               # what is in the session: tracks, buses, regions, plug-ins, automation
$ hypnomix project "Song/" --render      # render every track the way the session layers the regions
$ hypnomix scan "Song/"                  # the first pass: one review list (or point at Song.ardour)
$ hypnomix review "hypnomix-out/scan/Song" --serve   # play A and B, approve or skip
$ hypnomix als2ardour "Song.als" "~/HypnoMix/Song"   # a Live set into a NEW session folder; read IMPORT-REPORT.md

The DAW can stay open while you scan; nothing is written next to your session. A fix writes a new snapshot beside it; open that in the DAW when it is done. Any way of driving HypnoMix works with HypnoMix Studio sessions: choose your setup.
