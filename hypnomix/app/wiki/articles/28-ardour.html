What works today. hypnomix reads sessions saved by Ardour 3 to 9: tracks and buses, regions (with the way Ardour layers them: upper regions cover lower ones), fade curves, gain envelopes, multi-channel regions, plug-ins and automation. It renders each track from the session without opening Ardour, and scan runs the same checks as on a REAPER project: missing files, clicks at region edges, level jumps at comp cuts, vocal pitch and doubles, plug-ins switched off, the source recordings, boosted noise and "is this deliberate?" questions.

Fixes are written into a new snapshot. hypnomix fix "hypnomix-out/scan/Song" --approved --out "Song (fixed).ardour" writes one new .ardour with the changes you approved, next to your session file, inside the same session folder. That is where Ardour keeps its own snapshots, and where it looks for the session's audio (interchange/<session>/audiofiles), so the new file finds the same recordings; nothing is copied or moved, and your original is never touched. It works on every Ardour version hypnomix reads (sample times before Ardour 7, superclock and beat times after).

What it can write: region fades in and out (as a curve; shape=linear, power or slow in the decision note; a fade that is already longer is left alone), region gain, a dip over a spike (a quick turn-down in the region's gain envelope, so your fader and automation stay yours), the eased level after a comp cut (a gain envelope on the region that starts a little quieter and returns to normal; method=item sets the region gain instead), and fader, pan and mute on a track. A fader, pan or mute that already plays automation is refused. Track names and order, vocal stacks, timing nudges and sidechain stay by hand for now, and so does tuning (those make new audio you bring in). hypnomix handoff turns the by-hand list into a checklist in Ardour's words, with a MIDI marker file and a CSV to import.

Safety. It refuses when the session was saved after the scan, when the output exists, or on protected tracks. Each approved finding is found again by its region; one that is not there is skipped and said so. The new file is checked: same tracks, buses, regions, positions and lengths, ids unique, fade and envelope times well-formed, regions inside their files, and nothing outside the approved edits differs. A file that fails is renamed "(BROKEN, do not open)". Then fix reads it back, lists exactly what changed and measures the clips again.

Written and re-read by hypnomix; not yet opened in Ardour. Ardour is not installed where this was built, so nothing has been opened in a running Ardour. The reader was tested on real Ardour 3, 8 and 9 session files; the writer on synthetic sessions in both time formats. Please check in Ardour, and tell us what you see:

  • the new snapshot opens without a warning (Session > Open, or the snapshot list);
  • the audio is found (no missing-files dialog);
  • fades and gain are as written: the listed regions show the fade lengths and curve, the gain, and the envelope dips;
  • pan direction is the way round you expect (azimuth 0 is left), if you approved a pan move;
  • the trim knob is where it was (the fader move leaves it alone).

Findings that make new audio (tuning, de-hiss, de-ess) work as for any DAW. Driving the open session with Ardour's own plug-ins is next on the roadmap. The reader's renders also haven't been compared with Ardour's own export.

$ hypnomix project "Song/"               # what is in the session: tracks, buses, regions, plug-ins, automation
$ hypnomix project "Song/" --render      # render every track the way Ardour layers the regions
$ hypnomix scan "Song/"                  # the first pass: one review list (or point at Song.ardour)
$ hypnomix review "hypnomix-out/scan/Song" --serve   # play A and B, approve or skip

Ardour can stay open while you scan; nothing is written next to your session. A fix writes a new snapshot beside it; open that in Ardour when it is done. Any way of driving hypnomix works with Ardour: choose your setup.
