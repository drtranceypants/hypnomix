Three courses, one tab. Turn teacher mode on in the guided setup (the Your DAW step), or open the Teacher tab any time and pick a course. Each follows the DAW you chose, and the app remembers where you are.

courseforwhat's in it
Tour: hypnomix in your DAWpeople who already record and mix, and just need our softwarehow the loop works, connecting hypnomix to your DAW, the devices we've built for it (HypnomixProbe, HypnomixMain, Hypnomix and Hypnomix Align in Live; Hypnomix Probe, Hypnomix Main and the review panel in REAPER), the song card, the first pass, A/B inside the DAW, apply and undo, the tracking and mixing helpers, the mastering chain, review before export, working with an AI
The complete course, A to Zanyone who wants to learn the ropes of audio production, from nothing to a released song13 chapters and 138 to 146 short lessons, depending on your DAW (Live 146, REAPER 144, Ardour 138): sound and hearing (frequency, decibels, phase, mono and stereo, protecting your ears); digital audio (sample rate, bit depth, headroom, buffers, file types, backups); your room and your gear (interfaces, microphones, cables and levels, headphones against speakers, the room, starting mic positions); the words and the screen; the song before you record (tempo, key, arrangement, MIDI, loops); your first recording (monitoring, tuning up, voice, guitar and bass, takes); editing (comping, fades, clip gain, timing, gentle tuning, tidying, with the hypnomix tool for each); mixing (gain staging to the vocal chain, dynamics, space, each instrument, references, the ten most common mistakes); mastering (LUFS and true peak, streaming targets, the limiter, codecs, dither, albums); releasing (files, metadata, cover art, distributors, rights, archiving); when something goes wrong; cheat sheets; and the hypnomix tour. Most lessons have common mistakes, an exercise to try in your own DAW and a quiz that marks itself, and many have Hear it audio examples (two or more play buttons, made on the spot on your computer: a low cut, compression with a slow and a fast attack, a limiter against clipping, and about twenty more). The Glossary button lists every word, A to Z, with a filter and a link to the lesson that teaches it.
The 101 crash coursepeople just getting into audio productionthe words and the screen of your DAW, then a whole song: a tempo, recording to a click, a drum beat to play over (optional), a track armed with the right input, setting the input gain, recording takes; balance and pan, then each effect as you add it (EQ, compression, de-essing, reverb and delay on sends, saturation, groups, automation), what it does, why, and where to start; then what mastering is and a good mastering chain. It ends with the tour. It is the shortest way through (37 steps in Live, 35 in REAPER, 29 in Ardour), and every step of it is also in the complete course.

Live, REAPER and Ardour each get their own walkthrough. Ableton Live has the most depth (Session View, take lanes, the Max for Live devices), REAPER a little less, and Ardour the short version: hypnomix reads and scans Ardour sessions but can't drive Ardour yet, so every Ardour step shows where to click.

Let hypnomix do the hands-on steps. In Live (through the AbletonOSC connection) and REAPER (through its bridge) a step can do the job for you: set the tempo, switch the click on, add a four-bar drum beat on a MIDI track (Live: a kit from the Browser; REAPER: ReaSynDr), make an audio track on the right interface input, armed, record, add an effect with starting settings, build the mastering chain, or make the devices. Each one first says exactly what it will change and waits for Do it; anything it couldn't set is listed for you to set by hand, and your DAW's undo takes it back. Check my level listens to your interface for eight seconds and says which way to turn the gain; it records nothing.

No AI needed. Every lesson is in the app. With the Assistant set up, Ask the Assistant opens a chat about the step you're on (nothing is sent until you press Send), and while teacher mode is on the Assistant teaches at your course's level.

The Wiki follows your DAW. Its pages for the DAW you chose come first, after Start here, and the other DAWs' pages move to the end. The courses are in the Wiki too, under each DAW: "The complete course: make a song from scratch in …" is the contents page and links to one article per chapter ("Sound and hearing", "Mixing", …), and "Guided tour: hypnomix in …" is the tour. One Glossary article lists every word. The audio examples play in the app's Teacher tab only.

Tested in Live 12.4 and REAPER 7.86. In Live, an audio track can only be armed once Live has an audio input: if Settings → Audio has no Audio Input Device, the track step says so. In REAPER the beat plays on ReaSynDr, REAPER's own simple drum synth (its voices sit on notes 32 to 35); load any drum plug-in on the track for a real kit. On Live's return A, the reverb step sets the Reverb that's already there instead of adding a second one.

Setup remembers your choice. The guided setup's Your DAW step offers "No thanks", the tour, the 101 crash course and the complete course; you can switch in the Teacher tab at any time, and your place in each is kept (steps keep the same names across courses). The audio examples are made when you press play, from sounds the app synthesises, so nothing is downloaded; they are cached for two weeks in ~/.hypnomix/cache/teach and the disk tidy clears them.

Live drum beats and new tracks need this version's AbletonOSC script: Setup → Ableton Live offers to update it (the old copy is kept as a backup), then restart Live.
