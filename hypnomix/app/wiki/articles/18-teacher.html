Two courses, one tab. Turn teacher mode on in the guided setup (the Your DAW step), or open the Teacher tab any time and pick a course. Each follows the DAW you chose, and the app remembers where you are.

The 101 crash coursepeople just getting into audio productionthe words and the screen of your DAW, then a whole song: a tempo, recording to a click, a drum beat to play over (optional), a track armed with the right input, setting the input gain, recording takes; balance and pan, then each effect as you add it (EQ, compression, de-essing, reverb and delay on sends, saturation, groups, automation), what it does, why, and where to start; then what mastering is and a good mastering chain. It ends with the tour.

Live, REAPER and Ardour each get their own walkthrough. Ableton Live has the most depth (Session View, take lanes, the Max for Live devices), REAPER a little less, and Ardour the short version: hypnomix reads and scans Ardour sessions but can't drive Ardour yet, so every Ardour step shows where to click.

Let hypnomix do the hands-on steps. In Live (through the AbletonOSC connection) and REAPER (through its bridge) a step can do the job for you: set the tempo, switch the click on, add a four-bar drum beat on a MIDI track (Live: a kit from the Browser; REAPER: ReaSynDr), make an audio track on the right interface input, armed, record, add an effect with starting settings, build the mastering chain, or make the devices. Each one first says exactly what it will change and waits for Do it; anything it couldn't set is listed for you to set by hand, and your DAW's undo takes it back. Check my level listens to your interface for eight seconds and says which way to turn the gain; it records nothing.

No AI needed. Every lesson is in the app. With the Assistant set up, Ask the Assistant opens a chat about the step you're on (nothing is sent until you press Send), and while teacher mode is on the Assistant teaches at your course's level.

The Wiki follows your DAW. Its pages for the DAW you chose come first, after Start here, and the other DAWs' pages move to the end. The courses are in the Wiki too, under each DAW: "101: make a song from scratch in …" and "Guided tour: hypnomix in …".

Tested in Live 12.4 and REAPER 7.86. In Live, an audio track can only be armed once Live has an audio input: if Settings → Audio has no Audio Input Device, the track step says so. In REAPER the beat plays on ReaSynDr, REAPER's own simple drum synth (its voices sit on notes 32 to 35); load any drum plug-in on the track for a real kit. On Live's return A, the reverb step sets the Reverb that's already there instead of adding a second one.

Live drum beats and new tracks need this version's AbletonOSC script: Setup → Ableton Live offers to update it (the old copy is kept as a backup), then restart Live.
