Two models, each doing what it is best at. The Assistant talks with you and runs HypnoMix: it scans, reads the review, explains each finding and applies what you approve. The Listener only listens: it hears a bounce of your song and writes notes by ear, with timestamps. You can use Claude for the chat and a Google Gemini model as the ear, or any other pair. No Claude Code or terminal needed: it is all in the app's Assistant tab.

1 · Set up the Assistant

Open the app (hypnomix app), go to the Assistant tab, choose an AI provider, paste its key and choose a model (List models shows what your key can use). Any of these work: Claude, OpenAI, Google Gemini, OpenRouter (many models on one key), Groq, Mistral, any OpenAI-compatible service, or a free model on your own computer with Ollama and no key at all.

For Claude the default model is claude-sonnet-5-5 (the picker marks it default); a model you have already saved is kept, and every other model stays selectable: List models, or type its name.

Then just ask: "do a first pass on this set", "what does the review say about the vocals?", "apply what I approved". Attach a set, a folder or a bounce with the buttons under the chat box. Reading and measuring run straight away: a short list of commands that only look (a scan, a measurement, a check, a listing of your history), with the plain options they take. Everything else shows the exact command with Run it and Don't and waits for your click: any command that writes a set or a file you name (--out, --json, --apply), every fix, undoing a version, saving preferences or your taste profile, a diagnosis that plays and records through Live, anything that talks to Live or REAPER, and any option the app does not recognise. A scan the Assistant writes into the app's own scan folder runs at once; a scan sent anywhere else waits for you. The card says exactly which command will run. Every write is still a new set next to your original. A Store install (store install, Mac only) waits for your click like the rest; when its download cannot be checked against a SHA-256, the card says in plain words that it is not verified, and the install goes ahead only if you approve that card.

The Assistant can read the text reports in your songs folder, plus your preferences, profile and taste files. It cannot read the file that holds your saved keys, and if a key ever turns up in a message or a result it is replaced by its last four characters before it is sent or saved. Text that comes from your own files (track names, findings, reports) is passed to the model marked as data, never as instructions. If you change a provider's address in the settings, the key saved for it is forgotten, so it can't be sent to the new address: enter the key again.

What it costs, and keeping it cheap. The line above the chat counts the tokens of the conversation and the cost so far: exactly for Claude (Claude Haiku's price is higher for a request whose prompt passes 100,000 tokens, and the count knows it), as an estimate for Gemini (its paid-tier price, which follows the dates of Gemini's own price table; the free tier costs nothing), and as tokens only for the other companies. The line is worded for the company the conversation is with, and what listening costs is added on its own (a free model on this computer with a paid listen reads "free, plus about $0.24 for listening"), because the Listener is another service. Every step re-reads the whole conversation, so as it grows the Assistant clears the full text of old results (a page of findings, a report, a listen) from what it re-reads, leaving a one-line note of what each was and how to fetch it again; the newest six, and every result the AI has not read yet, are kept and your files are untouched (a line says so once per message). When one message has used more than 400,000 input tokens (change the number in the settings) it stops and asks Keep going or Stop here, and it stops by itself if it calls the same tool with the same arguments three times in a row. The song list is capped at the 25 newest songs, with a line saying how many more there are. A report longer than 30,000 characters is read in pieces, and each piece says where it was cut and where to read on.

A scan the Assistant starts goes into the app's own scan folder (it is told to), so the song shows in the app and in its own song list.

The conversation is saved as it goes, so closing the app in the middle of a message loses nothing: when you come back, a step that had not finished is closed as "not run" and you can carry on. A step that fails for any reason comes back to the AI as an error line, never as a broken conversation, and a saved conversation that cannot be read is kept beside it as conversation.json.bad while the chat starts empty.

2 · Set up the Listener (optional)

Under the chat settings, click Set up a listener. Choose the service, paste that service's key, pick a model and Save. The Listener's key is kept apart from the Assistant's, so the two can be different companies.

  • Google Gemini: a key from aistudio.google.com/apikey. There is a free tier, enough for occasional listens; on the free tier Google may use what you send to improve its models, the paid tier does not.
  • OpenRouter: one key from openrouter.ai/keys for the same Gemini models and others, pay as you go.

List models shows only the models your key can send audio to, best first. The three suggested ones:

  • gemini-4-carbon (the default): the best ear in our test; slowest, a few minutes a song, about 5 cents.
  • gemini-4-hydrogen: nearly as good and faster, about 3 cents.
  • gemini-3.5-flash: under a minute and the cheapest, about a cent.

How we chose: eight Gemini models listened to the same 7½-minute song whose answers we knew (its tempo, when a line is first sung, when the guitars fade). Only Gemini 4 Carbon got all three; three others got the tempo wrong. Every model undercounted a big stack of harmonies, so don't trust a listener to count your vocal parts.

3 · Ask it to listen

Attach a bounce and ask something like "how does the chorus feel? is the vocal too far back?" or "listen to the whole song and tell me where it drags". Before anything is sent, the chat shows the song, its length, the model, where it goes, the tokens (the scan's review that goes along as text is counted) and an estimated cost in dollars, with Send it and Don't. A song longer than 12 minutes asks whether to send all of it or only its first 12 minutes, with the cost of each. If you have already heard this song with this model and question, nothing is asked and nothing is sent: the saved notes come back free, with a Listen again button for fresh ones. The notes come back in the chat with timestamps, and are saved next to the song's scan as listen - Song.md.

What the ear is good at: arrangement and energy (where attention sags), the balance between parts, whether the lead vocal sits in front, low-end feel, harshness in the mids, timing feel and performance.

What it can't judge: only a reduced mono copy is sent (Gemini hears audio as 16 kHz mono anyway), so stereo width, air above about 8 kHz and exact loudness stay HypnoMix's measurements. The listener is told so, and is given the scan's review so it can confirm or question it. Its notes are a second opinion: nothing in them becomes a change unless you approve it, and HypnoMix still keeps the human feel (it will not quantise or hard-tune because a listener suggested it).

4 · In the DAW (DAW mode)

In DAW mode, with the HypnoMix engine running, the Assistant also gets the engine's own tools: the session, the transport, tracks, regions, plug-ins, markers and MIDI. Only tools that just read run by themselves (a fixed list: anything else, including any tool the engine adds later, asks). Every call that would change the session is shown as a card: a plain title and sentence (Change a fader: Drums, from -1.5 dB to -3.5 dB), what it changes from and to, the exact step under a disclosure, what undo can do for it, and Approve / Cancel (in the DAW's Assistant column and in this tab). It runs only when you approve, and the approval is for that card's call: it carries a fingerprint of the call, the answer must bring it back, and just before the call is sent the arguments exactly as they go to the engine are checked against it and against what the card shows (if they differ, nothing is sent); only a real yes counts (Enter approves only while the Approve button has the focus; a held key or an Enter meant for the chat box approves nothing). When the call has run, the card says how it ended in both places: applied (and what the engine reads back), failed (with the engine's words, and whether it may still have happened), cancelled or expired. The card is the question: the Assistant proposes the change straight away instead of asking in words first, and after Cancel it says nothing changed and doesn't ask again. A change that could not work (a track or region that isn't there, a missing or wrong value, a plug-in the track doesn't have) is never shown as a card, and after you approve, the DAW view shows the result, regions and markers included. What undo can do. Only region, marker, loop-range, warp, take-comp and MIDI edits are steps in the engine's undo history (the AI can propose Undo, which asks first). A fader, pan, mute, solo, send level or plug-in setting is not in it: the card says the old value, and taking it back is another card. Adding or removing a plug-in or a send is not in it either, and a removed plug-in or send loses its settings (adding it again starts from the defaults), which the card says. On an engine with MIDI inputs and MPE, setting a track's MPE mode is one undo step; marking a MIDI port (kept in this computer's port settings, not the session) or connecting a track's MIDI input is not, and the card says how to put it back with another card. On an engine with automation, writing or clearing automation is one undo step (a write takes back its points and the playback state together); switching a lane's playback on or off is not (as in the editor), and a tempo or time-signature change is one undo step (audio keeps its time, MIDI follows the bars). Smooth automation. Before a card for level automation (a fader, trim or send) is shown, the app checks HypnoMix's rules: gentle S-curves of about 1.2 s, a line move of at most 3 dB (a trim at most 4 dB), never steeper than 7 dB/s, starting and ending on the line as it is, and never on a bus or group that also carries tracks that are not vocals (riding it would ride them too). That last rule covers plug-in parameters as well: no card moves a plug-in on such a bus (the AI is told to automate a vocal track's plug-in instead). A write that breaks one gets no card; the AI is told why and rides the vocal tracks instead. Clearing a stretch inside a level lane is refused too when it would join the points either side with a straight move. Removing or reordering tracks cannot be undone, saving writes the session file, and a bounce never replaces a file that is already there, whatever way its name is spelled (the AI is asked for a new name). What the project says is data. Track, plug-in and region names, the engine's errors and what a change reads back reach the AI fenced as data, with your home folder shortened to ~ and file paths cut to the file's name, so a track named like an instruction is read as a name (a closing tag spelled with spaces, capitals or invisible characters does not close the fence either). What it costs. Every step carries the tool definitions again, and the engine has 136 tools, 144 when it has the MIDI input and MPE tools, and 152 when it also has automation and the tempo map; those groups (mpe, automation, tempo) are offered only then, and only when asked for (the 111 it had before the 25 for takes, groups, cue slots and the meter stream came were about 17,600 tokens a step, measured on the engine's real tool list). A request now carries about 3,600 tokens of them: the reads and the everyday moves, plus the groups the open view needs (the mixer view adds the other plug-in tools, the Arrangement the region tools); the AI can ask for any other group (daw_more_tools) and it is there from the next step. With every group asked for, a provider's limit (128 tools in one request) is kept by leaving out the second forms of a tool first (the bars-and-beats form of a move whose sample form stays, say); if that is not enough (every group asked for on the 152-tool engine), the group asked for longest ago is put away whole, never a few of its tools, and a note in the chat says so (asking for it again brings it back and puts the next oldest away); the everyday tools, the open view's and the group asked for last are always kept. Descriptions are cut short, replies are capped at 8,000 characters (waveform peaks and plug-in lists are summarised), the whole-session read is one read per track, all in flight together (80 tracks and 20 seconds at the most, about 3,500 tokens at the most), and lists regions only for a session of twelve tracks or fewer, and the older results are cleared and the per-message allowance applies in the DAW exactly as in this tab. A question about the real cost of a conversation is answered by the cost line above the chat, not by these numbers. The tool list is the first block of the model's cached prompt, so changing it (asking for another group, or a view that needs one) rewrites the cached conversation once, at the cache-write price, rather than reading it at the cheaper cache price; a group once offered therefore stays offered for the rest of the conversation, unless the limit puts it away. Hear it on a card of the scripted demo, with an engine, really changes the open session (it counts as an unsaved change, and the card says LIVE NOW while you listen); the app holds what puts each value back and does it when the card is cancelled, approved or replaced, when the session is saved (from the Save button or Ctrl/Cmd+S, before a stop, restart, open or new, by the Review panel's scan or by the Assistant itself: a tried value is never written into your file), and when the page closes or reloads. It can also propose to scan the open session, exactly as Scan session in the Review panel does (save, bounce the mix, scan), so its findings show in the Review panel too, then read you the review. The scan folders it is shown have your home folder written as ~, so your login name never reaches a model. If the engine stops and comes back, the conversation goes on and the Assistant is told the session went back to its last save. While the engine bounces the mix (a scan does this), it refuses play, stop and moving the playhead, and the Assistant says so plainly instead of trying again. It works with any of the providers above (tried end to end with a free Google Gemini key, Gemini 3 Flash and 3.1 Flash-Lite, before the changes described in this section about the smaller tool list, the fences, the approval's fingerprint and Hear it, which were tested with a stand-in model and a stand-in engine only). It has not been tried with a real sound card, recording or the native File menu. The same conversation shows in both places. If the engine is a window the app only attached to, the Assistant uses it only when it answers with the engine's own name and has the engine's core tools; a stranger on that port is not read and nothing is sent to it. The name check is not authentication (a program of yours could say the same name), so nothing such a window wrote reaches the AI as words: only the engine's own tool names are offered, with this app's titles and schemas rebuilt from names, types and limits, and every change still needs your card.

When the AI service is busy. Free tiers often answer "high demand" or "too many requests". The Assistant tries again by itself (a line in the chat says how long it waits; Stop ends the wait). Stop also works while the AI is still thinking: it stops waiting at once, and an answer that arrives later is not used. When a daily quota is used up, waiting a minute will not help, so it says which quota ran out and when it resets, in the service's own words: pick another model (each has its own quota) or come back later. Any other refusal from the service is shown in its own words too, not just as an error number.

Without the app

$ uv pip install -e ".[assistant,critique]"        # from source, in the HypnoMix folder; the installed app already has both
$ hypnomix critique "Song.wav" --estimate                 # what would be sent, and roughly what it costs
$ hypnomix critique "Song.wav" --scan "hypnomix-out/scan/Song" --question "is the vocal too far back in the chorus?"
  Song.wav: 3.5 min of reduced mono audio, to Google Gemini (generativelanguage.googleapis.com), model gemini-4-carbon. About 7,000 tokens in and 1,500 back, roughly $0.01 (an estimate)…
  Send this? [y/N]

critique never uploads without a yes. In a terminal it first prints what it will send (the length, the model, the estimated tokens and cost, where it goes) and asks y/N. Anywhere else (a script, an AI agent's shell) it refuses unless you pass --yes, which is only for when you have said yes to exactly what --estimate shows. --estimate only prints and sends nothing. A song over 12 minutes is warned about (--estimate prints the warning too, with the token count of the whole file and of its first 12 minutes) and you choose the whole file or its first 12 minutes (--minutes N picks any length; an AI agent passes --minutes 12 unless you chose the whole file). Notes already heard for the same file, model, question and context are shown again for free (kept in the HypnoMix folder, cache/listen); --again listens afresh.

critique uses the Listener's key and model from the app, or GEMINI_API_KEY / OPENROUTER_API_KEY; --provider and --model choose for one run. Whatever you give --profile or --measure (a plain .md, .json or .txt file, not hidden, under 1 MB) and the review in the --scan folder is sent along as text, so it refuses anything else. The Assistant can't run critique itself: its only way to have a model hear a song is the Listener, with the click above.

Your keys and your audio. Keys are kept in ~/.hypnomix/ai.json, and each goes only to its own service. On a Mac only your account can read that file. On Windows the file sits in your own user folder, which Windows keeps to your account and the computer's administrators; HypnoMix adds no lock of its own there. The Assistant reads HypnoMix's text reports. Audio leaves your computer only when a Listener is set up and you click Send it, one song at a time.
