Two models, each doing what it is best at. The Assistant talks with you and runs hypnomix: it scans, reads the review, explains each finding and applies what you approve. The Listener only listens: it hears a bounce of your song and writes notes by ear, with timestamps. You can use Claude for the chat and a Google Gemini model as the ear, or any other pair. No Claude Code or terminal needed: it is all in the app's Assistant tab.

1 · Set up the Assistant

Open the app (hypnomix app), go to the Assistant tab, choose an AI provider, paste its key and choose a model (List models shows what your key can use). Any of these work: Claude, OpenAI, Google Gemini, OpenRouter (many models on one key), Groq, Mistral, any OpenAI-compatible service, or a free model on your own computer with Ollama and no key at all.

For Claude the default model is claude-sonnet-5-5 (the picker marks it default); a model you have already saved is kept, and every other model stays selectable: List models, or type its name.

Then just ask: "do a first pass on this set", "what does the review say about the vocals?", "apply what I approved". Attach a set, a folder or a bounce with the buttons under the chat box. Reading and measuring run straight away: a short list of commands that only look (a scan, a measurement, a check, a listing of your history), with the plain options they take. Everything else shows the exact command with Run it and Don't and waits for your click: any command that writes a set or a file you name (--out, --json, --apply), every fix, undoing a version, saving preferences or your taste profile, a diagnosis that plays and records through Live, anything that talks to Live or REAPER, and any option the app does not recognise. A scan the Assistant writes into the app's own scan folder runs at once; a scan sent anywhere else waits for you. The card says exactly which command will run. Every write is still a new set next to your original.

The Assistant can read the text reports in your songs folder, plus your preferences, profile and taste files. It cannot read the file that holds your saved keys, and if a key ever turns up in a message or a result it is replaced by its last four characters before it is sent or saved. Text that comes from your own files (track names, findings, reports) is passed to the model marked as data, never as instructions. If you change a provider's address in the settings, the key saved for it is forgotten, so it can't be sent to the new address: enter the key again.

What it costs, and keeping it cheap. The line above the chat counts the tokens of the conversation and the cost so far: exactly for Claude (Claude Haiku's price is higher for a request whose prompt passes 100,000 tokens, and the count knows it), as an estimate for Gemini (its paid-tier price; the free tier costs nothing), and as tokens only for the other companies. Every step re-reads the whole conversation, so as it grows the Assistant clears the full text of old results (a page of findings, a report, a listen) from what it re-reads, leaving a one-line note of what each was and how to fetch it again; the newest six are kept and your files are untouched. When one message has used more than 400,000 input tokens (change the number in the settings) it stops and asks Keep going or Stop here, and it stops by itself if it calls the same tool with the same arguments three times in a row. The song list is capped at the 25 newest songs, with a line saying how many more there are. A report longer than 30,000 characters is read in pieces, and each piece says where it was cut and where to read on.

A scan the Assistant starts goes into the app's own scan folder (it is told to), so the song shows in the app and in its own song list.

2 · Set up the Listener (optional)

Under the chat settings, click Set up a listener. Choose the service, paste that service's key, pick a model and Save. The Listener's key is kept apart from the Assistant's, so the two can be different companies.

  • Google Gemini: a key from aistudio.google.com/apikey. There is a free tier, enough for occasional listens; on the free tier Google may use what you send to improve its models, the paid tier does not.
  • OpenRouter: one key from openrouter.ai/keys for the same Gemini models and others, pay as you go.

List models shows only the models your key can send audio to, best first. The three suggested ones:

  • gemini-4-carbon (the default): the best ear in our test; slowest, a few minutes a song, about 5 cents.
  • gemini-4-hydrogen: nearly as good and faster, about 3 cents.
  • gemini-3.5-flash: under a minute and the cheapest, about a cent.

How we chose: eight Gemini models listened to the same 7½-minute song whose answers we knew (its tempo, when a line is first sung, when the guitars fade). Only Gemini 4 Carbon got all three; three others got the tempo wrong. Every model undercounted a big stack of harmonies, so don't trust a listener to count your vocal parts.

3 · Ask it to listen

Attach a bounce and ask something like "how does the chorus feel? is the vocal too far back?" or "listen to the whole song and tell me where it drags". Before anything is sent, the chat shows the song, its length, the model, where it goes, the tokens (the scan's review that goes along as text is counted) and an estimated cost in dollars, with Send it and Don't. A song longer than 12 minutes asks whether to send all of it or only its first 12 minutes, with the cost of each. If you have already heard this song with this model and question, nothing is asked and nothing is sent: the saved notes come back free, with a Listen again button for fresh ones. The notes come back in the chat with timestamps, and are saved next to the song's scan as listen - Song.md.

What the ear is good at: arrangement and energy (where attention sags), the balance between parts, whether the lead vocal sits in front, low-end feel, harshness in the mids, timing feel and performance.

What it can't judge: only a reduced mono copy is sent (Gemini hears audio as 16 kHz mono anyway), so stereo width, air above about 8 kHz and exact loudness stay hypnomix's measurements. The listener is told so, and is given the scan's review so it can confirm or question it. Its notes are a second opinion: nothing in them becomes a change unless you approve it, and hypnomix still keeps the human feel (it will not quantise or hard-tune because a listener suggested it).

Without the app

$ uv pip install -e ".[assistant,critique]"        # from source, in the hypnomix folder; the installed app already has both
$ hypnomix critique "Song.wav" --estimate                 # what would be sent, and roughly what it costs
$ hypnomix critique "Song.wav" --scan "hypnomix-out/scan/Song" --question "is the vocal too far back in the chorus?"
  Song.wav: 3.5 min of reduced mono audio, to Google Gemini (generativelanguage.googleapis.com), model gemini-4-carbon. About 7,000 tokens in and 1,500 back, roughly $0.01 (an estimate)…
  Send this? [y/N]

critique never uploads without a yes. In a terminal it first prints what it will send (the length, the model, the estimated tokens and cost, where it goes) and asks y/N. Anywhere else (a script, an AI agent's shell) it refuses unless you pass --yes, which is only for when you have said yes to exactly what --estimate shows. --estimate only prints and sends nothing. A song over 12 minutes is warned about (--estimate prints the warning too, with the token count of the whole file and of its first 12 minutes) and you choose the whole file or its first 12 minutes (--minutes N picks any length; an AI agent passes --minutes 12 unless you chose the whole file). Notes already heard for the same file, model, question and context are shown again for free (kept in the hypnomix folder, cache/listen); --again listens afresh.

critique uses the Listener's key and model from the app, or GEMINI_API_KEY / OPENROUTER_API_KEY; --provider and --model choose for one run. Whatever you give --profile or --measure (a plain .md, .json or .txt file, not hidden, under 1 MB) and the review in the --scan folder is sent along as text, so it refuses anything else. The Assistant can't run critique itself: its only way to have a model hear a song is the Listener, with the click above.

Your keys and your audio. Keys are kept in ~/.hypnomix/ai.json, readable by you only, and each goes only to its own service. The Assistant reads hypnomix's text reports. Audio leaves your computer only when a Listener is set up and you click Send it, one song at a time.
