Two models, each doing what it is best at. The Assistant talks with you and runs hypnomix: it scans, reads the review, explains each finding and applies what you approve. The Listener only listens: it hears a bounce of your song and writes notes by ear, with timestamps. You can use Claude for the chat and a Google Gemini model as the ear, or any other pair. No Claude Code or terminal needed: it is all in the app's Assistant tab.

1 · Set up the Assistant

Open the app (hypnomix app), go to the Assistant tab, choose an AI provider, paste its key and choose a model (List models shows what your key can use). Any of these work: Claude, OpenAI, Google Gemini, OpenRouter (many models on one key), Groq, Mistral, any OpenAI-compatible service, or a free model on your own computer with Ollama and no key at all.

Then just ask: "do a first pass on this set", "what does the review say about the vocals?", "apply what I approved". Attach a set, a folder or a bounce with the buttons under the chat box. Reading and measuring run straight away; anything that would write a set shows the exact command with Run it and Don't, and every write is still a new set next to your original.

2 · Set up the Listener (optional)

Under the chat settings, click Set up a listener. Choose the service, paste that service's key, pick a model and Save. The Listener's key is kept apart from the Assistant's, so the two can be different companies.

  • Google Gemini: a key from aistudio.google.com/apikey. There is a free tier, enough for occasional listens; on the free tier Google may use what you send to improve its models, the paid tier does not.
  • OpenRouter: one key from openrouter.ai/keys for the same Gemini models and others, pay as you go.

List models shows only the models your key can send audio to, best first. The three suggested ones:

  • gemini-4-carbon (the default): the best ear in our test; slowest, a few minutes a song, about 5 cents.
  • gemini-4-hydrogen: nearly as good and faster, about 3 cents.
  • gemini-3.5-flash: under a minute and the cheapest, about a cent.

How we chose: eight Gemini models listened to the same 7½-minute song whose answers we knew (its tempo, when a line is first sung, when the guitars fade). Only Gemini 4 Carbon got all three; three others got the tempo wrong. Every model undercounted a big stack of harmonies, so don't trust a listener to count your vocal parts.

3 · Ask it to listen

Attach a bounce and ask something like "how does the chorus feel? is the vocal too far back?" or "listen to the whole song and tell me where it drags". Before anything is sent, the chat shows the song, its length and the rough cost, with Send it and Don't. The notes come back in the chat with timestamps, and are saved next to the song's scan as listen - Song.md.

What the ear is good at: arrangement and energy (where attention sags), the balance between parts, whether the lead vocal sits in front, low-end feel, harshness in the mids, timing feel and performance.

What it can't judge: only a reduced mono copy is sent (Gemini hears audio as 16 kHz mono anyway), so stereo width, air above about 8 kHz and exact loudness stay hypnomix's measurements. The listener is told so, and is given the scan's review so it can confirm or question it. Its notes are a second opinion: nothing in them becomes a change unless you approve it, and hypnomix still keeps the human feel (it will not quantise or hard-tune because a listener suggested it).

Without the app

$ pip install "hypnomix[assistant,critique]"
$ hypnomix critique "Song.wav" --estimate                 # what would be sent, and roughly what it costs
$ hypnomix critique "Song.wav" --scan "hypnomix-out/scan/Song" --question "is the vocal too far back in the chorus?"

critique uses the Listener's key and model from the app, or GEMINI_API_KEY / OPENROUTER_API_KEY; --provider and --model choose for one run.

Your keys and your audio. Keys are kept in ~/.hypnomix/ai.json, readable by you only, and each goes only to its own service. The Assistant reads hypnomix's text reports. Audio leaves your computer only when a Listener is set up and you click Send it, one song at a time.
