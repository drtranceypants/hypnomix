Apple-silicon Macs only, so far. DAW mode needs the HypnoMix engine. There is one app and one installer for everyone, and the engine is not inside them: it is an add-on, fetched once from Setup on a click (Setup → Extras, or choosing HypnoMix Studio as your DAW), checked against a SHA-256 fingerprint before it is unpacked, and removable from the same place. The first engine, 0.1.0, is published for Apple-silicon Macs only. On an Intel Mac or on Windows it is not available yet: there is nothing to add, so the app downloads nothing and shows no Studio tab. The tab appears when an engine is found (the add-on, once added), when the app is attached to a running window, or when the app is started with --daw. Most of what is below was checked against the engine on its silent test backend; the end of this part says what has not been tried. When a newer version of the app needs a newer engine, the DAW view and Setup say so and offer it; the old one is kept until the new one has run once, then removed.

A guided tour. The DAW can show you around on its own screen, step by step and only if you want it: the Tour button at the top right lists First look, Edit a region, Mix, Scan and review, Ask the Assistant, and Save and export. See A guided tour inside the HypnoMix Studio.

One app. With the add-on added, the app has a DAW tab (hypnomix app --daw opens on it): HypnoMix Studio's window, laid out like Ableton Live but calmer. Arrangement and Session clips, a Mixer, the Review of HypnoMix's findings, a browser on the left (its Store entry opens the app's Store tab, for free plug-ins and sounds), one device chain along the bottom and the Assistant in its own column on the right. Songs, Assistant and Wiki at the top lead back to the rest of the app. Press ? for every shortcut. The shortcuts rest while a dialog, the engine panel or a text box has the focus; Tab moves between controls as it does everywhere, and Enter and Space press the control that has the focus (in a dialog, never a button you have not reached: the safe one is focused first).

The engine underneath. The sound is made by the HypnoMix engine: the DAW's audio engine with no window of its own. The app starts it only while the DAW view is open, with your session, keeps an eye on it (if it crashes, the app starts it again on the same session and the window reads it again; changes you had not saved are lost, and a note says so until you save) and stops it when you quit. If it ever ends by itself without crashing, the view says Engine stopped unexpectedly: your session stays on screen (never swapped for the demo song), edits wait, and the Start button at the top right opens it again as it was last saved. It never saves by itself: your session is saved only when you save it.

Saving. The Save button (⌘S, or Ctrl+S) saves the open session through the engine. The app notices every change that went through, whether you made it or the Assistant did, and shows that something is unsaved. Before anything that would lose it (Stop, Restart, Open, New, quitting the app, leaving the page) it asks Save, Don't save or Cancel; the native app's Quit asks the same (and waits for the engine to stop before it quits), and the updater's Install and restart is refused until you have saved. A session you have just made or opened starts clean. Changes a command makes straight in the engine count too: hypnomix tidy, order and duck with --engine, and hypnomix loops or variations with --cues. The app asks the engine itself whether anything is unsaved (at most every 2 seconds), so within a few seconds the Save button shows it, and Stop, Restart, Open, New and quitting ask first. Give a command a couple of seconds to be seen before you stop or quit. To point those commands at the app's engine, give them --engine URL: the address is shown in the engine panel. A HypnoMix Studio window the app only attached to is not covered: save in that window. Only looking at a session never makes it unsaved: the meters' switch to RMS waits until you change something.

The engine's licence. The engine is a separate program under the GNU General Public License (version 2 or later); the HypnoMix Studio article says what it is built on. HypnoMix itself stays MIT and contains none of its code; the app starts the engine as its own process and talks to it only over MCP on this computer. The add-on archive carries it unchanged with its licence text and a source notice, and the app checks that they are there before the engine is put in place. Its source is public at github.com/drtranceypants/hypnomix-daw.

Adding and removing it. Setup → Extras shows HypnoMix Studio with its state, Add HypnoMix Studio (only when an archive is published for your computer: today that is Apple-silicon Macs only) and Remove HypnoMix Studio (refused while the engine is running, whether the click or the command asks: close the DAW view first). The command line has the same, report first: hypnomix engine (status), hypnomix engine install --yes, hypnomix engine remove --yes. The engine goes into engine/<version>/ (today engine/0.1.0/) in the HypnoMix folder (~/.hypnomix, or the folder HYPNOMIX_HOME names). The address and the fingerprint come only from a small manifest shipped inside the app, never from a request; the download has a size limit and a deadline, the fingerprint is checked before anything is unpacked, a failed or interrupted add leaves nothing half-added and can simply be run again. The fingerprint is checked when the engine is added, not again when it is started: the app starts the program it finds in that folder (or the one HYPNOMIX_ENGINE or daw.json names), and no request or page can change which program that is; anyone who can write into your HypnoMix folder can already run programs as you.

The window's File menu. The Mac app's window always has a File menu with New Session… (⌘N: choose a folder and a name), Open Session… (⌘O: a session's .ardour file or its folder) and Import Project…. Its items are dimmed until HypnoMix Studio has been added. If a session is open with unsaved changes it asks first (Save, Don't save or Cancel). These menus have been type-checked by the compiler but not yet clicked through by a person in the installed app: treat them as untested. Next time it opens the session you had open last; the very first time it says to make or open one.

Sound (not yet tried on a real sound card). Only the engine's silent test backend has been run so far. On a Mac the app is written to start the engine on your default output (System Settings → Sound → Output) with no input, so the Mac doesn't stop to ask about the microphone, but nobody has yet listened to it through a real device, and recording has not been tried at all. If no audio device will start, or the device doesn't answer, the engine starts without sound instead and the view says so: the session, edits, waveforms and exports all still work. It stays silent until you choose another output and press Restart in the engine panel. If another program changes the sound device's sample rate while a session is open, the engine sets it back by itself; it stops with a clear message only if the device refuses or the two programs keep changing it.

No engine program on your computer? The app can use a HypnoMix window that is already open, with Preferences → Control Surfaces → MCPHttp switched on. Then you open sessions in that window, and the app never closes it.

What has not been tried. The DAW view was tested against stand-in engines in the unit tests and against the real engine on its silent test backend. Not tried: playback through a real sound card, recording through a real input, the native File menu and its panels clicked by a person, the add-on's download and unpacking from the published release on an installed app, signing and notarisation of the engine, real MPE controllers (the engine's MPE passed its automated tests only), and Intel Macs (there is no Intel engine). Round 2 (take lanes and comping, groups and sidechains, the meter stream, the Session view's cue slots, the A/B session, and the --engine / --cues forms of tidy, order, duck, loops and variations) was tested against stand-in engines in the unit tests and against a development build of the engine on its silent test backend with a made-up session. Not verified: the meter stream inside the Mac app's own window (a same-origin WebSocket under the page's connect-src 'self'; if that window refuses it, the meters fall back to polling) and its reconnect after an engine restart, any of it on Windows or with real audio hardware, and anything by ear (comp crossfades, cue launches, the A/B level match, the duck and sidechain settings).

$ hypnomix app --daw --session "Song/Song.ardour"  # ... and opens that session

The app's own bar stays on top. The row of tabs you see everywhere else in HypnoMix (Studio, Songs, Preferences, Assistant, Wiki, Teacher, Live, Tools, Setup) is above the Studio screen too, so every part of the app is one click away. Leaving the Studio screen asks first if the session has changes that are not saved.

The first time. The engine starts when a session is open. With none open yet, the DAW view asks for one: type a folder and a name (it suggests ~/Music/HypnoMix/My Song) and press New session, or type the path of a session you have and press Open session. In the Mac app, File → New Session… and Open Session… do the same with a file chooser.

The engine panel. Click the button at the top right (it reads No engine, Demo or the engine's state): the engine's state and session, Connect, and Start, Restart, Stop, Open (a session or its folder) and New (a folder and a name). With no engine the view says so and offers Start the engine, Look again and, as a choice of its own, Practice with the demo song: a made-up song that plays no sound, saves nothing and sends nothing, and is labelled Demo. It never takes the place of a session you have open: Show the demo song in the engine panel asks first. When the engine starts again (Restart, or after a crash) the view reads the session again as it was last saved.

The top bar and the status bar. The page never scrolls sideways: anything that does not fit in the top bar slides inside the bar, at any width (a tour scrolls the button it points at into view). In a window 1520 pixels wide or less the top bar makes room on its own: the DSP meter, the clock and the time signature are hidden, and the name at the left may shrink down to the HypnoMix mark. The status bar at the bottom says which engine is connected as HypnoMix engine and its version, with the number of tools, the sample rate and how the meters arrive (meters streaming, say).

The Assistant beside your session. Set an AI key once in the Assistant tab. With the engine running, the Assistant column is the real Assistant: it reads the session first (tracks, faders, plug-ins, regions, in one look) and shows every change it wants as a card: what changes, from what to what (Drums · fader 0.0 dB → −2.0 dB, read from the engine just then), the exact step, Approve and Cancel (Esc); each card says in a plain title and sentence what it does and what undo can do for it (region, marker and MIDI edits are engine undo steps; a fader, pan, mute, solo, send or plug-in change is taken back by setting the old value again, and removing or reordering tracks cannot be undone). Nothing changes until you click Approve (Enter works only while that button has the focus); after you do, the card says how it ended: what the engine reads back, or plainly why the engine refused. A card nobody answers expires after 10 minutes, and if the engine restarts while a card waits, that change is not made. Ask it to scan this song and it proposes what the Review panel's Scan session does (save the session, bounce the mix, run the HypnoMix scan), then explains the findings; they show in the Review panel as well. Without a key or an engine it is a scripted demo, marked as one.

The mixer and devices are the engine's. On your session the meters are the engine's own (peak over RMS, with a held peak; click the number to clear it), the playhead follows the engine's clock (both arrive about 20 times a second on the engine's meter stream, and the status line says meters streaming; with an engine that has no stream they are asked for instead, about 15 times a second; a stream that goes quiet is closed after 15 seconds and opened again, and opening a fifth DAW view closes the one open longest), the transport bar shows the master's loudness as one number (short-term LUFS, the last 3 seconds) and the status bar at the bottom shows the rest (M, momentary LUFS; TP, true peak in dBTP; C, stereo correlation; they go away while the stream is down, so no old number looks live), and every fader, pan, mute, solo, arm and send you move is read back from the engine a moment later. Changes made elsewhere (by the Assistant, say) show up within a few seconds. In the Mixer, click a track's small colour square to recolour it, drag its name sideways to move it, and use × to delete it (it asks first: the engine cannot undo a deleted track). The device chain shows the track's real plug-ins: knobs set their parameters, the light bypasses, drag a title to move a device, × twice removes it (the Remove? stays up for about 4 seconds, or until the pointer leaves it), and + (or a double-click in the browser) adds one from the engine's list, the Hypnomix Probe, Main and Bass Mono devices included. Adding or removing a plug-in is one undo step: ⌘Z after an add removes the plug-in that was added, and after a remove puts the same plug-in back at the same place with the parameter values it had. The Probe's readings (loudness, peaks) update while it is shown.

Group tracks and sidechains. A group works as in Live: Group… in the Arrangement's tools (or ⌘G) asks for a name and which tracks (every selected track is ticked: shift-click a track header for a range, ⌘-click, or Ctrl-click on Windows, to add or take away one; shift-dragging a fader or knob is still the fine drag and keeps the selection), then the engine makes a group with its own bus. By keyboard, Tab goes round the dialog's name box, tick boxes and buttons, and Space types a space in the name or ticks a box. A track folded away in a group is never the selected one: the arrow keys skip it, and folding a group selects its header. The tracks are routed to the bus, the bus sits right above them and its fader is the group fader; nothing else about the tracks changes (their own faders, mutes and solos stay theirs). ▾ on the group's header folds its tracks away and ▸ brings them back; the fold is saved with the session. Ungroup (on the header when you point at it, or Tab to it) sends the tracks to the master again and removes the bus. A compressor (or any plug-in that can listen to another track) shows Sidechain with a list of tracks: pick the kick to key the bass from it, none to stop. The key is taken after that track's own plug-ins and before its fader, like Live's "Post FX". Grouping and ungrouping ask first, because the engine cannot undo them; a sidechain choice is undone with ⌘Z like a fader move. The scan's sidechain and duck checks measure these compressors on your session, and hypnomix tidy and hypnomix order can make the groups for you (only the ones you name with --ids; nothing is written without it).

The Arrangement is the real session. Each region is drawn from the engine's own waveform data, finer as you zoom in, at its real place and length, with its name, gain, fades and mute, all read back from the engine; a region moved, trimmed, muted, faded or turned up by another client shows up within a few refreshes (the view looks at every track's regions every 3 seconds, and at a few regions' fades and gain each time round). Edit as in Live: drag a clip to move it (onto another track too; hold ⌘ for no snapping), drag its edges to trim, drag the small handles at its top corners for fades, drag the thin gain line up or down for clip gain, click where you want a cut and press ⌘E to split, 0 mutes and ⌫ deletes. Each edit is one undo step in the engine, and ⌘Z / ⇧⌘Z use the engine's own undo; if something else changed the session since (the Assistant, say), the view says so and undoes nothing.

Takes and comping. A track can keep several takes beside the comp it plays, like Live's take lanes: each recording pass lands on the comp and is also kept as a new take, and the Assistant can make an empty take for an import. A track with takes has a T button on its header: it shows the takes as lanes under the track (Take 1, Take 2, …), with the parts the comp plays lit. Drag across a take to comp that stretch onto the track: the take plays there, with a 10 ms crossfade centred on each end, and the rest of the comp stays as it was. Each comp is one step to undo (⌘Z). By keyboard, Tab to a take's Comp button: it asks for the bars (the loop range to start with) and makes the same comp. The comp's pieces are ordinary regions that still play the take's own recording, so every fix afterwards is an ordinary clip edit on them (an edge, a fade, clip gain), and the takes themselves are never changed.

Automation lanes. On an engine with automation, every track has an A button on its header: it lists what can be automated (the fader, trim, pan, width, mute, each send and each plug-in parameter) and shows a lane under the track for each one you tick. A lane draws the points and the line between them; the line is grey while the lane is off (manual) and the Play / Off button on the lane's header switches playback. Drag a point to move it, double-click a lane to add a point on the line (nothing you hear changes until you drag it), and ⌥-click a point to remove it. By keyboard, Tab to a lane's header: ← and → pick a point (the playhead goes there), ↑ and ↓ move it (0.5 dB, with ⇧ 0.1 dB), A adds a point at the playhead, Delete removes the picked one and P plays or stops the lane. Each of these is one step to undo (⌘Z). Level lanes (the fader, trim and sends) keep to HypnoMix's smooth-automation rules: every move is a gentle S-curve, at most 3 dB (a trim 4 dB) and never steeper than 7 dB/s, so a drag stops where a rule would be broken, and the first and last points of a level lane keep their value (the way into a move is an S-curve from the line). A lane's many small points inside an S-curve are drawn, not dragged: the square handles are where the line turns or is flat. The app checks the same rules again before a change reaches the engine, and never automates the level or a plug-in parameter of a bus or group that carries anything but vocals (moving it would move those tracks too: automate the vocal tracks, or a vocal track's plug-in, instead; a bus that feeds it is judged by what it carries, not by its name; removing a point is still allowed); what it refuses is put back and the reason shown.

The tempo track. The Tempo button above the lanes shows the session's tempo map as a track at the top: each tempo, and a ramp as a slope from its tempo to the one it reaches at the next point, labelled (100→140). The bar grid still counts at the session's first tempo. Tempo changes are made with the Assistant, hypnomix tempomap --follow or hypnomix breathe; audio keeps its time when the tempo changes, MIDI follows the bars.

Importing audio. Drop files from the Finder onto a track (they go onto it, at the drop point) or below the tracks (one new track each). A web page cannot see where a dropped file lives, so the page sends the file to the app, which keeps it in a temporary folder until the engine has copied it into the session. Or click Import… and paste the files' full paths (in the Finder, ⌥⌘C copies them).

Importing a project from another DAW. Type the project's path in the engine panel and press Import (on the Mac, File → Import Project… does the same once HypnoMix Studio has been added). It brings a .als, .logicx, .dawproject or .rpp project across as a new session, "name (HypnoMix)", beside the project (or beside its folder, for a project that keeps its audio in its folder); .ptx sessions and .aaf exports need two readers that come with the add-on. Your project is only read and never changed. Only audio inside the project is copied, unless you tick Also copy audio from outside the project (in the engine panel, or in the File menu's panel). When it is done the view offers to open the new session (asking about unsaved work first, like Open). What each file type carries and leaves out is in Bring a project from another DAW across. The import itself does not need DAW mode: hypnomix import PROJECT OUTDIR works from a terminal.

Review: the HypnoMix loop on your session. With the engine running, Review (⌘4) scans the session that is open. Scan reads the saved session, so when you have unsaved changes it asks Save and scan (it never saves unless you click that); then it bounces the mix and runs the HypnoMix scan on it, with its progress in the view (a few minutes). If you change the session while the mix is being bounced, the scan stops and says so, because its findings would no longer match what you hear. Each finding shows its track, where it is, what Apply will do with it and its evidence (the level of its track and of the mix around the spot); selecting one moves the playhead there and selects the track. Approve, Skip and Change (with a note) are saved as you click, the same decisions as everywhere else in HypnoMix. Apply asks first, then writes your approved fixes into one new snapshot beside the original, Song (hypnomix).ardour: region fades, cuts, spike dips and mixer moves. The original snapshot is never written. Open snapshot then switches the engine to it. Repaired recordings (de-ess, breaths) are made as new files for you to put in, tuning stays by hand for now, and each finding says which it is.

Session view on the engine. The Session view (Tab) shows each track's 16 real clip slots. Click a clip to launch it: it starts on the next bar (and starts the transport if it is stopped), and a clip already playing on that track hands over on that bar line. Click an empty slot or a track's stop button to stop its clip on the bar, a scene to launch that whole row, keys 1–8 for scenes 1 to 8. To fill a slot, select a track and drag one of its regions from the strip under the grid onto a slot (a copy goes in: trimming the region later does not change the clip), or drop an audio file from the Finder on it; the × on a clip clears the slot. By keyboard: Tab goes into the slots (the whole grid is one Tab stop, scenes and stop buttons included), the arrow keys move between them as the grid looks (left and right between tracks and on to the row's scene, up and down between slots and on to the stop buttons), Enter or Space launches the focused slot, scene or stop button and Delete or Backspace clears a slot, as × does. Moving onto a track's slot selects the track, so the strip under the grid shows its regions; Tab to a region there, press Enter to pick it, then Enter on a slot puts it in (Esc lets go). A screen reader reads each slot as the track, the slot number and the clip (or empty), and whether it is playing or queued. Launching and stopping do not mark the session unsaved (they change nothing that is saved). With less motion asked for in the system settings, the slots' lights hold still. While any slot of a track holds a clip (launched or not), that track's Arrangement does not play; this is how the underlying engine works. Clear its last clip and it plays again at once. So loops go on a track of their own. hypnomix loops SESSION.ardour --track Drums --cues and hypnomix variations SESSION.ardour --cues fill slots for you, on a track of their own next to the part (a new one, or the empty slots of one already there with that name). Save the session to keep the clips.

A/B. Repaired recordings and tuning can be heard before and after (Build A/B, level-matched). For clip and mixer fixes (fades, cuts, spike dips, fader, pan and mute moves) press Build A/B session: HypnoMix writes one new snapshot beside yours, Song (A-B), where each of those fixes is ready as a B (a fixed copy of the track's regions, or the new mixer values). Open A/B session (it asks first: the engine closes the open session, and what you have not saved there is not kept), then on each finding A and B (or the B key) switch it in the engine while it plays, and Blind plays X and Y without saying which is which until you choose (your choices are kept with the scan). Saving, scanning, leaving Review and closing the page put A back first, so B is never saved or left playing by mistake. The louder side is turned down on the master fader by the measured difference, so you hear the change, not the level; that difference is measured on a rough mix without plug-ins (muted tracks left out, so muting or unmuting a track is matched too), so a compressor after the change can move it. It is turned down by at most 12 dB: when a fix silences most of what plays at the spot (muting the only track there, say), matching it fully would leave you listening to near silence. The note under A and B then gives the measured difference and says it is turned down by 12 dB, the most it is. Building it again makes a numbered snapshot and keeps the first one reviewable. The A/B session plays exactly like the original until you press B, and Apply still writes the real fixes from the original.

While an export runs. When the mix is being bounced (Review's scan does this, and so can the Assistant), the engine will not play, stop or move the playhead until the bounce is done, so it cannot be cut short. The transport buttons are greyed out, the bar says Exporting with how far it has got, and pressing Space or clicking in the timeline says why. Region and mixer edits wait too, so the bounce is of the mix you scanned: a fader, clip or plug-in you change while it runs goes back where it was and the view says An export is running: edits wait until it finishes. Everything comes back by itself when it is done.

What is real now. On your session the meters, the playhead, the mixer and devices, the regions with their gain and fades, and every waveform come from the engine; the engine draws the waveforms of new imports itself (for a moment a region says it is building its waveform). The engine keeps 200 undo steps.

Still being built. The DSP load is not shown, changing the tempo works only on the demo song, region gain envelopes are not drawn, and the loop and record buttons show what you last set here (the engine cannot be asked for them yet; turning the loop on while stopped starts playing the loop). When a track's plug-ins are moved, or changed elsewhere, undo steps for that track's device settings are dropped (the add / remove step itself checks the plug-in is still where it was before it undoes anything).

Private. Your browser talks only to the app, and the app only to the engine on this computer. Requests from other sites are refused. The Assistant sends text to the AI service you chose, never your audio unless you set up a Listener and click Send it.
