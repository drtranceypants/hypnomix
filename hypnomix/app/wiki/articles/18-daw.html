Not available yet. DAW mode needs the Hypnomix engine, and only the full download carries it; that download is not released. In an ordinary install the app shows no DAW tab (Setup → Extras explains what the Hypnomix DAW is and that it is not available yet). The tab appears when an engine is found, when the app is attached to a running Hypnomix window, or when the app is started with --daw. Everything below describes the local test build.

One app. In DAW mode the hypnomix app opens on the DAW tab: the Hypnomix DAW's window, laid out like Ableton Live but calmer. Arrangement and Session clips, a Mixer, the Review of hypnomix's findings, a browser on the left (its Store entry opens the app's Store tab, for free plug-ins and sounds), one device chain along the bottom and the Assistant in its own column on the right. Songs, Assistant and Wiki at the top lead back to the rest of the app. Press ? for every shortcut. The shortcuts rest while a dialog, the engine panel or a text box has the focus; Tab moves between controls as it does everywhere, and Enter and Space press the control that has the focus (in a dialog, never a button you have not reached: the safe one is focused first).

The engine underneath. The sound is made by the Hypnomix engine: the DAW's audio engine with no window of its own. The app starts it with your session, keeps an eye on it (if it crashes, the app starts it again on the same session and the window reads it again; changes you had not saved are lost, and a note says so until you save) and stops it when you quit. If it ever ends by itself without crashing, the view says Engine stopped unexpectedly: your session stays on screen (never swapped for the demo song), edits wait, and the Start button at the top right opens it again as it was last saved. It never saves by itself: your session is saved only when you save it.

Saving. The Save button (⌘S, or Ctrl+S) saves the open session through the engine. The app notices every change that went through, whether you made it or the Assistant did, and shows that something is unsaved. Before anything that would lose it (Stop, Restart, Open, New, quitting the app, leaving the page) it asks Save, Don't save or Cancel; the native app's Quit asks the same (and waits for the engine to stop before it quits), and the updater's Install and restart is refused until you have saved. A session you have just made or opened starts clean.

The engine's licence. The engine is a separate program under the GNU General Public License (version 2 or later): the Hypnomix DAW is a rebranded fork of Ardour, a free DAW. hypnomix itself stays MIT and contains none of its code; the app starts the engine as its own process and talks to it only over MCP on this computer. The full download carries it unchanged with its licence text and a source notice. The source will be published with the first release that includes the engine; there is no public source address yet.

In the full Hypnomix app. The full download (a local test build for now, not released) is one app with the engine inside it: it opens on this view, and its File menu has New Session… (⌘N: choose a folder and a name) and Open Session… (⌘O: a session's .ardour file or its folder). If a session is open with unsaved changes it asks first (Save, Don't save or Cancel). These menus have been type-checked by the compiler but never run by a person: treat them as untested. Next time it opens the session you had open last; the very first time it says to make or open one.

Sound (not yet tried on a real sound card). Only the engine's silent test backend has been run so far. On a Mac the app is written to start the engine on your default output (System Settings → Sound → Output) with no input, so the Mac doesn't stop to ask about the microphone, but nobody has yet listened to it through a real device, and recording has not been tried at all. If no audio device will start, or the device doesn't answer, the engine starts without sound instead and the view says so: the session, edits, waveforms and exports all still work. Choose another output, then press Restart in the engine panel.

No engine program on your computer? The app can use a Hypnomix window that is already open, with Preferences → Control Surfaces → MCPHttp switched on. Then you open sessions in that window, and the app never closes it.

What has not been tried. The DAW view was tested against stand-in engines in the unit tests and against the real engine on its silent test backend. Not tried: playback through a real sound card, recording, the native File menu and its panels, the full app's .dmg from a download, signing and notarisation, and Intel Macs.

$ hypnomix app --daw                               # the DAW view first; starts (or finds) the engine
$ hypnomix app --daw --session "Song/Song.ardour"  # ... and opens that session

The engine panel. Click the button at the top right (it reads No engine, Demo or the engine's state): the engine's state and session, Connect, and Start, Restart, Stop, Open (a session or its folder) and New (a folder and a name). With no engine the view says so and offers Start the engine, Look again and, as a choice of its own, Practice with the demo song: a made-up song that plays no sound, saves nothing and sends nothing, and is labelled Demo. It never takes the place of a session you have open: Show the demo song in the engine panel asks first. When the engine starts again (Restart, or after a crash) the view reads the session again as it was last saved.

The Assistant beside your session. Set an AI key once in the Assistant tab. With the engine running, the Assistant column is the real Assistant: it reads the session first (tracks, faders, plug-ins, regions, in one look) and shows every change it wants as a card: what changes, from what to what (Drums · fader 0.0 dB → −2.0 dB, read from the engine just then), the exact step, Approve and Cancel (Esc); each card says in a plain title and sentence what it does and what undo can do for it (region, marker and MIDI edits are engine undo steps; a fader, pan, mute, solo, send or plug-in change is taken back by setting the old value again, and removing or reordering tracks cannot be undone). Nothing changes until you click Approve (Enter works only while that button has the focus); after you do, the card says how it ended: what the engine reads back, or plainly why the engine refused. A card nobody answers expires after 10 minutes, and if the engine restarts while a card waits, that change is not made. Ask it to scan this song and it proposes what the Review panel's Scan session does (save the session, bounce the mix, run the hypnomix scan), then explains the findings; they show in the Review panel as well. Without a key or an engine it is a scripted demo, marked as one.

The mixer and devices are the engine's. On your session the meters are the engine's own (peak over RMS, with a held peak; click the number to clear it), the playhead follows the engine's clock, and every fader, pan, mute, solo, arm and send you move is read back from the engine a moment later. Changes made elsewhere (by the Assistant, say) show up within a few seconds. In the Mixer, click a track's small colour square to recolour it, drag its name sideways to move it, and use × to delete it (it asks first: the engine cannot undo a deleted track). The device chain shows the track's real plug-ins: knobs set their parameters, the light bypasses, drag a title to move a device, × twice removes it (the Remove? stays up for about 4 seconds, or until the pointer leaves it), and + (or a double-click in the browser) adds one from the engine's list, the Hypnomix Probe, Main and Bass Mono devices included. Adding or removing a plug-in is one undo step: ⌘Z after an add removes the plug-in that was added, and after a remove puts the same plug-in back at the same place with the parameter values it had. The Probe's readings (loudness, peaks) update while it is shown.

The Arrangement is the real session. Each region is drawn from the engine's own waveform data, finer as you zoom in, at its real place and length, with its name, gain, fades and mute, all read back from the engine; a region moved, trimmed, muted, faded or turned up by another client shows up within a few refreshes (the view looks at every track's regions every 3 seconds, and at a few regions' fades and gain each time round). Edit as in Live: drag a clip to move it (onto another track too; hold ⌘ for no snapping), drag its edges to trim, drag the small handles at its top corners for fades, drag the thin gain line up or down for clip gain, click where you want a cut and press ⌘E to split, 0 mutes and ⌫ deletes. Each edit is one undo step in the engine, and ⌘Z / ⇧⌘Z use the engine's own undo; if something else changed the session since (the Assistant, say), the view says so and undoes nothing.

Importing audio. Drop files from the Finder onto a track (they go onto it, at the drop point) or below the tracks (one new track each). A web page cannot see where a dropped file lives, so the page sends the file to the app, which keeps it in a temporary folder until the engine has copied it into the session. Or click Import… and paste the files' full paths (in the Finder, ⌥⌘C copies them).

Review: the hypnomix loop on your session. With the engine running, Review (⌘4) scans the session that is open. Scan reads the saved session, so when you have unsaved changes it asks Save and scan (it never saves unless you click that); then it bounces the mix and runs the hypnomix scan on it, with its progress in the view (a few minutes). If you change the session while the mix is being bounced, the scan stops and says so, because its findings would no longer match what you hear. Each finding shows its track, where it is, what Apply will do with it and its evidence (the level of its track and of the mix around the spot); selecting one moves the playhead there and selects the track. Approve, Skip and Change (with a note) are saved as you click, the same decisions as everywhere else in hypnomix. Apply asks first, then writes your approved fixes into one new snapshot beside the original, Song (hypnomix): region fades, cuts, spike dips and mixer moves. The original snapshot is never written. Open snapshot then switches the engine to it. Repaired recordings (de-ess, breaths) are made as new files for you to put in, tuning stays by hand for now, and each finding says which it is.

A/B. Repaired recordings and tuning can be heard before and after (Build A/B, level-matched). Clip and mixer fixes have no A/B in an Ardour session yet: apply them to the new snapshot and compare it with the original.

While an export runs. When the mix is being bounced (Review's scan does this, and so can the Assistant), the engine will not play, stop or move the playhead until the bounce is done, so it cannot be cut short. The transport buttons are greyed out, the bar says Exporting with how far it has got, and pressing Space or clicking in the timeline says why. Region and mixer edits wait too, so the bounce is of the mix you scanned: a fader, clip or plug-in you change while it runs goes back where it was and the view says An export is running: edits wait until it finishes. Everything comes back by itself when it is done.

What is real now. On your session the meters, the playhead, the mixer and devices, the regions with their gain and fades, and every waveform come from the engine; the engine draws the waveforms of new imports itself (for a moment a region says it is building its waveform). The engine keeps 200 undo steps.

Still being built. The DSP load is not shown, launching clips and changing the tempo work only on the demo song, region gain envelopes are not drawn, and the loop and record buttons show what you last set here (the engine cannot be asked for them yet; turning the loop on while stopped starts playing the loop, as in Ardour). When a track's plug-ins are moved, or changed elsewhere, undo steps for that track's device settings are dropped (the add / remove step itself checks the plug-in is still where it was before it undoes anything).

Private. Your browser talks only to the app, and the app only to the engine on this computer. Requests from other sites are refused. The Assistant sends text to the AI service you chose, never your audio unless you set up a Listener and click Send it.
